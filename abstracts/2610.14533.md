# Sample-Size Scalability and Representation Sensitivity of Quantum Machine Learning Models for Higgs Boson Event Classification

**Authors:** Shiksha Bhardwaj, Ankhi Roy

Quantum machine learning offers new approaches to multivariate event classification in high-energy physics, but its performance relative to optimized classical methods remains uncertain under limited training data and restricted quantum resources. We investigate the sample-size scalability and representation sensitivity of a quantum support vector machine and a variational quantum classifier for Higgs-boson signal--background discrimination using simulated events from the ATLAS Higgs Machine Learning Challenge. The models are trained on balanced subsets ranging from 250 to 4000 events and evaluated on held-out samples preserving the original class distribution. Principal component analysis (PCA) and autoencoder (AE) representations are examined to reduce the input dimensionality and quantum-resource requirements. Four optimized classical classifiers are used as benchmarks under a common preprocessing, validation, and evaluation protocol. We assess training-sample scalability in classical and quantum classifiers using a four-component PCA representation and examine representation sensitivity through a secondary matched-event comparison of four- and eight-component PCA representations. Within the evaluated Higgs-boson classification setting, the QSVM showed stronger performance than the VQC at larger training sample sizes, while remaining below the best-performing classical classifiers.
