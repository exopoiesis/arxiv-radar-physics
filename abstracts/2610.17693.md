# Extreme events and statistics in an AI emulator of a global storm-resolving model

**Authors:** Anna Kwa, W. Andre Perkins, Troy Arcomano, Noah D. Brenowitz, Christopher S. Bretherton, Spencer K. Clark, Lucas M. Harris, Jeremy McGibbon, Michael S. Pritchard, Oliver Watt-Meyer

We present HiRO-ACEv2 (High Resolution Output Ai2 Climate Emulator), a two-stage framework for AI emulation of the 3~km resolution X-SHiELD global storm-resolving model (GSRM), and highlight its ability to generate large ensembles of data for studying the characteristics of tropical cyclones and extreme events in high-resolution simulations at an inference computational cost over 300 times lower than that of traditional numerical GSRMs. HiRO-ACEv2 expands upon the precipitation-only HiRO-ACEv1 model to generate coherent precipitation, 10~m winds, and sea level pressure outputs, enabling a broader range of events to be simulated at high-resolution. Fine-resolution predictions are generated by running an autoregressive emulator of coarsened X-SHiELD at 100 km, whose multivariate outputs are regionally downscaled to 3~km resolution via a separately trained diffusion model. Particular importance is placed on capturing the statistics of extreme events during the diffusion downscaling step. Our ability to match the tails of distributions and generate realistic-looking extreme events in both the `perfect prediction' case as well as in free-running emulator rollouts is improved via the use of multiple denoisers at inference time and several updates to the data sampling, noise sampling, and loss weighting during training. HiRO-ACEv2 closely matches the high-resolution reference distributions of precipitation and near-surface winds out to their 99.9999th percentile values, and broadly tracks the reference distribution of tropical cyclone intensities.
