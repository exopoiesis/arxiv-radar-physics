# Identifying Scientifically Interesting Galaxies with Vision-Language Models

**Authors:** Yixuan Cheng, Nolan Koblischke, Jo Bovy

Anomaly searches in large astronomical image datasets aim to identify rare objects for follow-up investigation. Yet the objects that are most statistically unusual are not necessarily those of greatest scientific relevance. We investigate whether pretrained vision-language models can directly assess scientific interest from natural-language instructions, enabling galaxy images to be ranked without labeled examples. We evaluate models on a 20,000-image Hubble Space Telescope sample that includes 167 reference anomalies previously identified through machine learning and expert inspection. By scoring images for scientific interest, Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite places 98.2% of the reference anomalies within the highest-ranked ~2,000 images, at a present-day inference cost of approximately $5.05 per 10,000 images. We recover known gravitational lenses, jellyfish-galaxy candidates, and galaxies with visible jets, and release 102 high-scoring candidates for further investigation. These results show that an off-the-shelf vision-language model, without any training on the target data, offers a practical way to rank large image datasets by scientific relevance rather than rarity. The project code is available at [https://github.com/TheCheng2005/Galaxy-Anomaly-Search/](https://github.com/TheCheng2005/Galaxy-Anomaly-Search/)
