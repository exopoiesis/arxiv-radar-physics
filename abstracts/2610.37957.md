# Analytical framework for interpreting thermoreflectance measurements of nanoline gratings under finite spot illumination

**Authors:** Abhishek Pathak, Seungwon Park, Shubham Deshmukh, Alan J. H. McGaughey, Jonathan A. Malen

A frequency-domain thermoreflectance (FDTR)-based technique is presented to measure the effective thermal properties of a material stack whose surface is patterned with metallic nanoline gratings that are heated and probed by lasers. An analytical heat diffusion model is derived for predicting the FDTR phase response that incorporates a finite laser spot radius with a Gaussian intensity profile for the pump and probe lasers, as well as the dimensions of the nanoline grating. The model is validated by comparing with measurements of Au nanoline gratings, with widths ranging from 100 to 1000 nm, fabricated on a-SiO$_2$ and $c$-sapphire substrates. Due to the short phonon mean free paths (MFPs) in these substrates, thermal transport remains in the diffusive regime for the whole range of nanoline widths. The model and the measurement show excellent agreement across different laser spot radii and nanoline widths, with a bulk thermal conductivity value of $c$-sapphire and the thermal boundary conductances at the Au-Cr/a-SiO$_2$ and Au-Cr/$c$-sapphire interfaces comparable to our prior measurements and the literature. The validated model will serve as a robust baseline for extending this method to measure effective thermal conductivity as a function of the heater size. This approach is particularly relevant for materials where non-diffusive thermal transport regimes enable probing phonon MFP dependent contributions to the thermal conductivity.
