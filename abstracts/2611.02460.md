# New Wine in an Old Bottle -- NeuralPOM: A Hybrid Ocean Forecast Model with Differentiable Dynamics and Staged Learning

**Authors:** Ruiqi Shu, Guangliang Liu, Ruijian Gou, Tianrun Gao, Haomin Wen, Hao Li, Xiaomeng Huang

Coarse-grid ocean circulation models rely on empirical subgrid parameterizations that struggle to capture mesoscale eddies, whereas purely data-driven neural forecasters accumulate trajectory drift, violate tracer conservation, and damp small-scale variability over extended integrations. Here we present NeuralPOM, a hybrid ocean forecast model that couples differentiable explicit mode-split and semi-implicit free-surface implementations of the Princeton Ocean Model (POM) to a conservation-constrained neural corrector. The three-dimensional primitive-equation solver advances the ocean state, while the neural network periodically updates the physical fields at high-frequency (1 h) or low-frequency (1 day) intervals under volume, heat, and salt conservation constraints. Using a fine-grid reference dataset exceeding 100,000 model days in a wind-driven double-gyre basin, we introduce a three-stage training procedure comprising offline single-step pretraining, multi-step differentiable training through the ocean solver, and multi-day closed-loop rollout training. Over 100-day forecasts, NeuralPOM reduces errors across all prognostic fields relative to coarse-grid physical closures and direct neural forecasters, preserving mesoscale kinetic energy while improving eddy phase alignment. Controlled ablations show that closed-loop training suppresses multi-day drift, whereas backpropagating gradients through the differentiable solver stabilizes high-frequency coupling. Finally, because high-frequency tracer updates can introduce density anomalies via the equation of state that excite spurious baroclinic pressure gradients, we introduce Density-Normal Damping (DND) to attenuate the density-modifying (diapycnal) tracer component while retaining the density-compensated (isopycnal) correction.
