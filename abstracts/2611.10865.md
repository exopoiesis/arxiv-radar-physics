# Neural Operator Surrogates for Landweber Iteration in Solving Inverse Problems

**Authors:** Kuo Gai, Marco A Iglesias, Shuai Lu, Frank Werner

Neural operators can accurately approximate well-posed mappings, but for unbounded or discontinuous operators, common in inverse problems, we verify that their network parameters often blow up in architectures such as DeepONet and FNO. To solve inverse problems stably, we use Landweber iteration, which requires only a forward map and the adjoint of its derivative (linearisation). To reduce repeated PDE forward evaluations, we replace the forward operator and its derivative with a differentiable neural-operator surrogate. We show that encoder-network-decoder operators, including generalised DeepONets and fully coupled Fourier operators, approximate both the forward map and its derivative. A modified discrepancy principle ensures decreasing error before finite stopping. With a compact preconditioner, the iterates remain in a compact set where accurate surrogates exist, and the stopped reconstructions converge as data and surrogate errors vanish under a regularity condition. We also relate the theory to derivative-informed training along finite directions, showing that the preconditioner determines which derivative errors affect the Landweber iteration update. Experiments on identifying a reaction coefficient in an elliptic equation show that smoothing reduces late-iteration deterioration for less accurate surrogates but slows sharp-feature recovery, and that smaller derivative errors along training directions do not by themselves improve reconstruction accuracy.
