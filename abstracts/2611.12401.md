# Data-Driven Conservation Laws for Newtonian Systems: Physics-Informed Neural Networks and Eisenhart-Duval Geometry

**Authors:** Andronikos Paliathanasis

We introduce a Physics-Informed Neural Network approach for the data-driven reconstruction of the symmetries and conservation laws of Newtonian dynamical systems, based on the geometrization of the dynamics through the Eisenhart-Duval lift. Within the Eisenhart-Duval lift, the symmetry conditions reduce to the linear Killing or Conformal Killing equations of the extended metric. We combine a Hamiltonian neural network, which reconstructs the potential function from the data, with a random-feature neural network, which solves the symmetry conditions for the collineations of the extended geometry. From the learned vector fields we construct the Lie algebra and, through Noether's theorem, the corresponding conserved quantities, while the derivation of the curvature invariants for the extended geometry provide allow us to perform an independent consistency test. We apply the method to the one-dimensional Ermakov-Pinney equation and to the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator, with and without a quartic perturbation, by using different level of noise in the phase-space data. For the Ermakov-Pinney equation, the three Killing vectors of the flat extended plane, and the corresponding nonlocal conservation laws, are recovered for, while for the harmonic oscillator, the ten conformal Killing vectors which form the $so(3,2)$ Lie algebra are determined for noise levels up to $10\%$. When the quartic term is added, the broken symmetries are identified as approximate symmetries.
