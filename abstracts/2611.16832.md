# Certifying Majorana persistence through Hamiltonian learning from boundary measurements

**Authors:** Tohid Farajollahpour, Alireza Qaiumzadeh

Finite measurements at a chain end can certify local quantum dynamics without determining bulk topology. We combine Hamiltonian learning with commutator bounds to certify the persistence of localized Majorana operators in Kitaev chains. Time-resolved single-shot boundary measurements and local bond calibrations define joint confidence sets for local Hamiltonian coefficients. Under explicit device assumptions, these sets yield state-independent lower bounds on symmetrized autocorrelations with a prescribed failure probability, valid for all compatible continuations. With equal hopping and pairing on the first bond and no additional boundary terms, an exact identity enables inference from the end qubit alone, even with arbitrary time-independent interactions elsewhere. Synthetic benchmarks near a boundary sweet spot certify persistence for up to about 60 times the measurement window. Three- and six-qubit IBM records strengthen conditional guarantees for the programmed coherent Hamiltonian. Applications to quantum-dot models relate tuning precision to persistence times and detuning-induced splitting.
