# AeroZoo: A Benchmark of Well-Posed Flow Problems for Foundation Models of Aerodynamics

**Authors:** Peter Sharpe, Carmelo Gonzales, Corey Adams, Mohammadmehdi Ataei, Sanjay Choudhry

Neural surrogates for computational fluid dynamics (CFD) are trained and ranked on single-family, fixed-domain datasets that cannot test the minimum bar for an aerodynamic foundation model: predicting CFD-quality incompressible flow in seconds for new shapes in new enclosures. On DrivAerML, larger models and longer training barely lower Transolver's error; doubling training cases cuts it by about 12% up to 400 cases, exhausting the dataset. We introduce AeroZoo, a benchmark and training corpus of 3,815 time-mean unsteady Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes (URANS) solutions with a transition model across seven geometry families, eight enclosures, and Reynolds numbers from $10^3$ to $10^8$. Each sample poses a well-posed PDE problem as a solver receives it: boundary meshes grouped by condition type, prescribed values, and fluid properties. The 2,816 time-mean-stable samples form the canonical benchmark: two tasks, mean engineering metrics with an overall flow score $S_{\mathrm{flow}}$ (1.000 for uniform inlet flow), and 22 frozen geometry-disjoint splits holding out shapes, enclosures, boundary-condition layouts, and Reynolds ranges. On the random split, boundary-condition tokens cut GeoTransolver's mean velocity error from 89.8% to 15.2% of inlet speed (uniform flow: 44.0%; $S_{\mathrm{flow}} = 0.290$). ISLA, a boundary-driven transformer, reaches 8.2% ($S_{\mathrm{flow}} = 0.153$; 0.123 with boundary targets), about 6 times mesh-refinement sensitivity. On shared test samples, holding out a geometry family barely changes ISLA's mean velocity error, whereas holding out enclosures or boundary-condition layouts raises it 3.8 to 8.4 times. The dataset is public; the scoring code will be released.
