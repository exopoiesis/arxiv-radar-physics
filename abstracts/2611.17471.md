# Resilient Transmission of Highly Structured Light via Active Convolved Illumination

**Authors:** Anindya Ghoshroy, James Davis, Martin Wilkens, Sahin K. Ozdemir, Durdu O. Guney

Wave propagation through lossy and noisy systems as well as spatiotemporally dynamic random media often limits the complex information that can be reliably transmitted or retrieved. Progress in adaptive optics, wavefront shaping, machine learning, and wave propagation theories has significantly advanced physical and computational correction techniques. However, many approaches remain constrained by restrictive operating conditions, limiting their ability to meet growing application-specific demands, such as higher spatial resolution in imaging and greater data capacity in communications, particularly in highly dynamic environments. Recently, active convolved illumination (ACI) was proposed as a novel framework for preserving the spatial structure of optical modes during propagation through strongly distorting environments, such as atmospheric turbulence. ACI aims to bypass the prohibitive complexity of global distortion tracking by combining spectrally localized characterization with compensation using a correlated auxiliary source. Here, we provide a comprehensive review of ACI, detailing the formulation of correlation-injecting and auxiliary sources and the role of segmented reciprocal-space characterization and distortion compensation. We explore extensions in free-space optical communication and potential high-precision phase measurement, and numerically evaluate interferometric strategies for experimental implementation. This work highlights ACI as a potentially versatile framework for distortion-resilient light transmission, with broad interdisciplinary utility ranging from atmospheric physics and remote sensing to telecommunications, target tracking, and biomedical diagnosis, offering flexible deployment either as a standalone paradigm or hybridized with existing optical systems.
