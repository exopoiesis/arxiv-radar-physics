# Foundation Tensor Networks Generalize Across Lattice Hamiltonians

**Authors:** Ryan T. Grimm, Michael Lindsey

Neural quantum states and tensor networks have emerged as leading techniques for simulating quantum many-body systems. However, disordered systems remain extremely challenging in both frameworks as they require computation of the ground state for every realization of the disorder. Recently, neural quantum states have been used to construct foundation models that generate the ground state directly from the Hamiltonian. Still, they are hindered by the need to perform extensive sampling to estimate observables. To address these difficulties, we introduce the foundation tensor network (FTN), which uses a neural network to generate the tensor network representation of the ground-state wavefunction from the Hamiltonian. To accelerate training, we develop a technique, called \texttt{GridBP}, based on iterated one-dimensional belief propagation that reduces the cost of computing the energy expectation value to just two tensor contractions. We use FTNs to calculate the critical parameters of the glass transition in the quantum Edwards-Anderson model via finite-size scaling analysis. To facilitate rapid averaging, we use a moment-generating function to evaluate the order parameter, lowering the cost to a single tensor contraction. We obtain high-fidelity estimates for the critical parameters by averaging over 500,000 disorder realizations.
