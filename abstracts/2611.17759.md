# Convergence analysis of physics-informed neural networks for subdiffusion

**Authors:** Bangti Jin, Qiqi Rao, Zhi Zhou

Physics-informed neural networks have emerged as mesh-free solvers for partial differential equations, yet their rigorous convergence theory for time-fractional problems remains limited. We present a convergence analysis of PINNs for linear subdiffusion equations and for the associated backward problem of recovering the initial data from terminal observations. To address the nonlocality and weakly singular kernels of fractional derivatives, we introduce a discretization-based PINN framework built on some popular time-stepping schemes satisfying discrete maximal regularity, including backward Euler, BDF2, L1, and fractional CN schemes. This approach avoids the exact evaluation of fractional derivatives at training points while preserving the stability structure needed for convergence analysis. For the forward problem, we prove that, under the natural fractional maximal-regularity condition $u\in W^{α,p}(0,T;L^2(Ω))\cap L^p(0,T;H^2(Ω)\cap H_0^1(Ω))$, the piecewise linear temporal reconstructions of PINN-loss minimizers converge to the exact solution. This result avoids the higher-order regularity assumptions on the exact solution that are often imposed in existing PINN convergence analyses. We also verify the required discrete maximal regularity and consistency properties for the aforementioned popular time discretizations. We then extend the analysis to the backward subdiffusion problem and establish convergence of the regularized recovery of the initial data from terminal observations, despite its ill-posedness. Numerical experiments for both forward and backward problems support the theoretical results, demonstrate the necessity of regularization, and show that $L^2$-type losses alone are generally insufficient. These results establish a rigorous theoretical basis for discretization-based PINNs for time-fractional diffusion problems.
