# Tracing Uncertainties of Extreme El Niño Prediction to Mesoscale Ocean Currents

**Authors:** Bo Qin, Zeyun Yang, Qiaofei Xue, Yanan Ding, Jinyu Wang, Chaopeng Ji, Shijin Yuan, Bin Mu, Wansuo Duan, Guokun Dai, Mu Mu, Yi Deng

The predictability of the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) has historically been linked to large-scale errors in models' initial conditions such as heat content and surface wind stress. Here we demonstrate that these slowly evolving errors may ultimately originate from mesoscale errors in initial oceanic states that are common in current models and ocean observation systems. This hidden source of ENSO forecast uncertainty, in terms of mesoscale ocean-current errors, is convincingly exposed by the conditional nonlinear optimal perturbation (CNOP) method applied to a differentiable ocean AI model. The CNOP-identified most influential current errors are strongly event-dependent. For the 1997/98 El Niño, the initial current errors in the eastern Pacific results in the largest forecast error through locally suppressing original subsurface warming. For the 2015/16 event, the dominate error originates from the western Pacific, which reorganizes upper ocean temperature structure and affects the eastern Pacific through the equatorial waveguide. Further analysis reveals that these current errors perturb mesoscale eddy count and eddy kinetic energy triggering large-scale temperature errors that hinder El Niño development. The timing of the maximum growth rate of the obtained CNOP error is well aligned with the spring predictability barrier of ENSO, and the error structures are reflected in simulated analysis errors caused by unresolved mesoscale currents. These findings extend the potential source of ENSO predictability beyond large-scale thermal signals, reveal for the first time an uncharted route of ENSO forecast failure, and provide an important physical basis for targeted ocean observations and data assimilation in the future.
