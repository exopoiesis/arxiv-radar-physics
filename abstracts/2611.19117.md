# Testing General Relativity by Reconstructing Dark Energy and the Growth Index with Physics-Informed Neural Networks

**Authors:** Andronikos Paliathanasis

We extend the Cosmo-PINN, a physics-informed neural network for model-independent cosmological reconstruction, to reconstruct the dark energy equation of state parameter $w_{\mathrm{DE}}(z)$ and the growth index $γ(z)$ of matter perturbations. The reconstruction is performed through three modules: the background module, the matter growth module, and the growth index module. The background module learns the dark energy density $ρ_{\mathrm{DE}}(z)$ from cosmological observations, and $w_{\mathrm{DE}}(z)$ is reconstructed through the residual of the conservation equation. The reconstructed background is then passed to the other two modules, which reconstruct the observables $f(z)$ and $fσ_{8}(z)$ either directly from the linear perturbation equation of General Relativity or through an independently reconstructed growth index $γ(z)$. Comparing the two reconstructions on the same background provides a consistency test of the underlying physical framework. The network is trained on the DESI DR2 Baryon Acoustic Oscillations, Cosmic Chronometers, Type Ia Supernovae, and redshift-space distortion data. Posterior uncertainties for the reconstructed cosmological parameters and functions are obtained through Hamiltonian Monte Carlo sampling. For the different data combinations, the posterior median of the present-day dark energy equation of state lies within the range $w_{\mathrm{DE,0}}\in \lbrack -0.930,-0.867]$, while the phantom divide crossing takes place at $z\simeq 0.41$--$0.53$. The posterior median of the present-day growth index reconstructed from the data lies within $γ_{0}\simeq 0.60$--$0.62$, indicating suppression of the growth of cosmic structure.
