# Towards a foundation model for Type IIB flux vacua

**Authors:** Sven Krippendorf, Magdalena Larfors, Zhimei Liu, Andreas Schachner, Moritz Walden

Progress in characterising the Type IIB flux landscape requires scalable methods for constructing flux vacua. In this work, we investigate generative models as a framework for sampling vacua across multiple Calabi-Yau geometries and for directing searches towards specified physical properties. We compare transformers and diffusion models trained to generate quantised fluxes together with approximate values of the complex structure moduli and axio-dilaton. We exploit a staged training procedure comprising pre-training, and post-training via reinforcement learning, and assess how each stage improves the generated distributions and enables targeted sampling. We further examine whether structures learned from geometries with smaller $h^{1,2}$ can be transferred to geometries with larger $h^{1,2}$ through fine-tuning on specialised datasets. Our results provide evidence that generative models can learn distributions of flux vacua across distinct compactifications, improve searches for vacua with selected properties and transfer information between geometries. This establishes a concrete basis for developing a foundation model of the Type IIB flux landscape.
