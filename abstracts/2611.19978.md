# GPROF V08: Improving Satellite Precipitation Estimates Across a Heterogeneous Constellation of Passive-Microwave Sensors

**Authors:** Simon Pfreundschuh, Paula J. Brown, Christian D. Kummerow

Precipitation estimates from the passive microwave observations (PMW) of the Global Precipitation Measurement (GPM) mission serve a wide range of scientific, societal, and economic applications. The Goddard Profiling Algorithm (GPROF) is the operational precipitation retrieval used to produce the level-two precipitation estimates for the GPM mission. This study presents GPROF V08 - the most recent version of GPROF and a major update aimed at improving the accuracy of precipitation estimates from the GPM constellation.   GPROF V08 replaces the single-pixel Bayesian retrieval scheme used by earlier GPROF versions with a convolutional neural network (CNN). Implementing the algorithm for the full GPM constellation required addressing several challenges such as the generation of synthetic observations suitable for training CNN retrievals, which has been described in the first part of this two-part study. This second part describes the final, operational implementation of GPROF V08, which adds a finetuning stage to the training to overcome remaining uncertainties in the synthetic training data. We extensively validate the new GPROF V08 algorithm for principal GPM sensors against reference precipitation estimates from combined radar and radiometer retrievals from GPM and TRMM as well as independent ground-based reference measurements. While the magnitude of improvements varies by sensor and surface type, the retrieval accuracy for all assessed sensors is improved.   Although challenges such as the persistence of climatological biases (which remain of the same order as for GPROF V07) persist, GPROF V08 constitutes a substantial advancement in spaceborne precipitation monitoring. Since the improvements are robust across all assessed GPM sensors, the update is expected to improve the full historical GPM precipitation record and thus benefit a wide range of downstream datasets and applications.
