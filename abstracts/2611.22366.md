# Embedding or bridging? AI4Science teams differ more in knowledge recombination than in problem novelty

**Authors:** Yun Fu, Jielan Ding

AI for Science (AI4Science) is reshaping scientific research, yet how the organization of AI-related and domain-scientific work relates to different dimensions of research performance remains poorly understood. We analyze 489,409 AI4Science articles published between 1990 and 2025, identifying seven affiliation-based organizational configurations comprising three forms of embedding and four forms of cross-unit bridging. Focusing on 2006-2025, we examine two dimensions of research performance: knowledge creation, characterized by scientific problem novelty and knowledge-recombination breadth, and research impact, assessed through journal placement and citation impact. We find that organizational configurations differ only modestly in problem novelty but substantially in knowledge-recombination breadth. Cross-unit bridging is consistently associated with broader knowledge recombination, particularly in AI-oriented bridging, without a corresponding advantage in problem novelty. However, greater recombination breadth does not necessarily align with stronger research impact. Science-oriented and Multi-orientation bridging exhibit the most consistent positive associations with JCI Q1 journal placement and citation impact, although neither shows a statistically significant adjusted advantage in publication in the Elite-4 journals (Nature, Science, Cell, and PNAS). These research-impact differences remain largely intact after accounting for problem novelty and recombination breadth. Our findings reveal that organizational configurations are associated with distinct patterns of knowledge creation and research impact, rather than a single hierarchy of research performance. The results highlight the importance of considering organizational arrangements in relation to specific scientific objectives, rather than assuming that any one configuration is universally superior.
