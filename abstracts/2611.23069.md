# RevolIACT: a longitudinally distributed array of compact AI-driven Cherenkov telescopes for the time-domain sub-TeV sky

**Authors:** David Paneque, Elli Jobst, Lukas Heinrich, Alexander Hahn

Context. The coming decade will be the golden era of time-domain astronomy. In ten years, however, sensitive very-high-energy observations below ~1 TeV may be possible only at the two sites of the Cherenkov Telescope Array Observatory (CTAO). Each can observe a source for only a few hours per night. Aims. We quantify the loss caused by this limited coverage and assess whether RevolIACT, an array of compact stand-alone AI-driven stations, can close the gap. Methods. We estimate the sensitivity that a stand-alone station needs to detect important transients seen by current imaging atmospheric Cherenkov telescopes (IACTs). We also compute the temporal coverage of layouts of ten to fourteen stations distributed in longitude, and how many of the 4401 Fermi-GBM gamma-ray bursts each layout could have observed when they occurred. Results. For each station, a sensitivity of ~15 % of the Crab flux in one hour above 0.2-0.3 TeV, about three times worse than that of current IACT systems, would suffice for an array to detect most of these transients. With the standard analysis, a telescope with a 5 m diameter mirror misses this target by a factor of 17. With a video vision transformer, it comes within a factor of two in an idealised simulation, and a 6 m telescope within 1.5. Ten to fourteen stations would provide three to four times the temporal coverage of CTAO, averaged over the sky and for the GBM bursts. Fourteen stations can observe most sources around the clock for about two months per year. Conclusions. Temporal coverage, not only flux sensitivity, will limit sub-TeV transient astronomy in the next decade. RevolIACT would complement CTAO. The concept relies on the transformer analysis, which is still young and will improve. The decisive next step is to confirm this performance on real data, with a prototype station.
