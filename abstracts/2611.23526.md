# Linking Dynamical Time Scales and Experimental Design to Parameter Identifiability from Time-Series Observations

**Authors:** Sara Shamekh, Viviana Acquaviva

Parameter estimation in chaotic models often relies on prescribed summary statistics, whose ability to constrain parameters may be difficult to establish in advance. Different parameters influence different features and time scales of the dynamics, so the record length and sampling interval needed to constrain them may differ. We choose to learn a representation directly from the trajectory and use it as our summary statistic. The recurrent encoder that produces this representation is trained jointly with a conditional normalizing flow for amortized posterior estimation. We use the Lorenz-63 and two-scale Lorenz-96 systems to investigate how inference depends on the observed variables, record length and sampling interval, and compare our results with the state-of-the-art Calibrate--Emulate--Sample method. In Lorenz-96, learned summaries of a 2 model-time-unit (MTU) record achieve estimation skill comparable to prescribed second-moment statistics from substantially longer records for several parameters. Fast-process parameters remain weakly constrained by slow-variable records across the tested 2 to 100 MTU windows, while adding fast-field averages improves their recovery from 2 MTU. Coarser sampling weakens inference from fast-field observations, but longer coverage can partly compensate. Once trained for a given observation setting, the estimator we develop returns posteriors for compatible records without further model integration. These proof-of-concept results illustrate how data-driven learning of summaries can be used to inform the design of observations in dynamical systems.
