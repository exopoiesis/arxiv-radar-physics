# Asteroseismology of AI Velorum: a prototype of high-amplitude delta Scuti stars

**Authors:** Przemysław Walczak, Jadwiga Daszyńska-Daszkiewicz, Wojciech Niewiadomski, Wojciech Szewczuk

AI Velorum is exceptional among high-amplitude δScuti (HADS) stars because of its multimode pulsations. Despite being an excellent target for asteroseismology, this prototype star has not been studied in detail.   We determine the stellar parameters, age, and evolutionary stage of AI Vel, constrain envelope convection and convective core overshooting, and test three opacity data sets: OPAL, OP, and OPLIB.   We analysed TESS photometry, ground-based Strömgren photometry, and HARPS spectroscopy. Modes were identified using photometric and spectroscopic methods, and empirical values of the non-adiabatic f-parameter were derived for both radial modes. Seismic modelling was performed within a Bayesian framework supported by Monte Carlo simulations.   We confirm the radial nature of the two dominant modes and identify one non-radial mode as most likely an axisymmetric dipole or quadrupole mode. Within the explored parameter space, only OPAL models reproduce the observed position of AI Vel in the HR diagram, whereas OP and OPLIB models are too cool and underluminous. All seismic models favour inefficient envelope convection (mixing-length parameter alpha_{mlt}\lesssim 0.5). Including the non-radial mode substantially reduces the formal posterior uncertainties, but the ambiguity in its identification introduces additional model-dependent uncertainties. AI Vel is an evolved, slowly rotating star in the hydrogen shell-burning phase.   Our results demonstrate the diagnostic power of combining pulsation frequencies with empirical f-parameters. The preference for OPAL opacities is consistent with results for several other HADS and SX Phoenicis stars but does not imply a general superiority of OPAL opacities.
