# Invertible Transport Between Sample-Only Distributions via Projected Characteristic Functions

**Authors:** Juan Gutiérrez, Victor Gutiérrez-García, José Luis Blanco-Murillo

Many useful latent distributions can be sampled but do not have a tractable density. Such distributions may be represented by empirical collections of real-world observations or generated by scientific simulators and black-box generative models. These sample-only priors cannot be used directly in likelihood-based normalizing flows, which require evaluating the prior density. We propose Projected Characteristic-Function matching (PCF), a density-free framework for learning an invertible map between a data distribution and a prior specified only through samples.   Our method aligns distributions using projected empirical characteristic functions, eliminating the need for density estimation, adversarial training, or paired observations. To address the non-uniqueness of the transport map, we introduce an optional transport regularizer that selects lower-displacement transformations while preserving endpoint alignment.   We benchmark PCF on controlled transports, sample-efficiency studies, and high-dimensional alignment tasks. PCF matches the accuracy of kernel MMD on the controlled benchmark and on raw-pixel MNIST--USPS at a cost linear in the batch size, and it is more sample-efficient, achieving lower endpoint error than MMD in 13 of 15 paired runs across library sizes. By avoiding pairwise kernel evaluation, it reduces discrepancy memory by 29.8% and trains at batch sizes where kernel MMD exceeds GPU memory. These results show that PCF provides accurate and efficient invertible transport, particularly in sample- and memory-limited settings.
