# Eigenspectra of Poisson-sampled stellar population models: Statistical fingerprints of cool giant stars in unresolved early-type galaxies

**Authors:** Anastasia Gvozdenko, Russell J. Smith

The light of unresolved galaxies carries the imprint of their underlying stellar populations, yet isolating contributions from specific stellar types remains challenging. If multiple spectra of the same population are available, e.g. from high-resolution integral field unit (IFU) observations, random sampling variation can be exploited to extract information beyond that carried by the integrated light. We develop a principal component analysis (PCA) approach, applied to Poisson-sampled stellar population models, to probe the most luminous cool giant stars in old galaxies. Across all wavelengths (0.45--2.4\,$μ$m), and over all ages and metallicities explored, Poisson-sampling variation are dominated by a single PCA component. This leading component typically describes the most massive and coolest giant stars in the population. In the infrared, it resembles the integrated-light spectrum since the latter is already dominated by such stars; even so, all features from warmer stars are greatly suppressed. In the optical, by contrast, the method instead reveals cool M-giant contributions which are subdominant in the integrated light. A second PCA component is present in some bands, which is related to the variation in spectrum along the giant branch. Our model predictions are largely insensitive to age or the initial mass function, but show some dependence on metallicity, reflecting changes in the temperature and shape of the giant branch. The method can be applied to IFU observations in a purely data-driven way; comparison to our predictions will provide a new approach to testing some of the uncertain ingredients in stellar population models for early-type galaxies.
