# A sliding mode observer perspective on chaos synchronisation of turbulent flows

**Authors:** Defne E. Ozan, Andrew Wynn

State estimation of turbulent flows is challenging as infinitesimal prediction errors grow exponentially due to chaos. However, when signals from one chaotic system (the master) are injected into an identical system with different initial conditions (the slave), the slave can synchronise with the master --- a phenomenon observed in turbulent flows. From a control-theoretic perspective, this synchronisation problem can be viewed as observer design. We first show that synchronisation by direct substitution of observed variables into the slave system is equivalent to a sliding mode observer. This perspective enables the analytical verification of stability as well as the design of feedback for robustness. We focus on reduced-order Navier--Stokes models with quadratic non-linearities, a broad class of systems for which stability can be analysed by seeking Lyapunov functions through parameter-dependent linear matrix inequalities solved via semidefinite programming. Extending this framework, we develop a neural network approach combining a neural ordinary differential equation with a decoder to learn data-driven mappings from observation histories to companion forms, where observer design is greatly simplified. Results are demonstrated on the Lorenz system and a nine-dimensional reduced order model of a turbulent shear flow. This work opens opportunities in the rigorous development of observers for turbulent flows.
