# Diffusing the assumptions: towards realistic LISA stochastic component separation with physics-aware simulation-based inference

**Authors:** Iuliu Cuceu, Nelson Christensen, Astrid Lamberts, Quentin Baghi, Coen Rondeel, Natalia Korsakova

Simultaneous global-fit inference of the numerous sources observed by the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) depends on accurate modeling of its stochastic components: instrumental noise, the Galactic foreground, and astrophysical or stochastic gravitational wave backgrounds (SGWB). Realistic features such as non-Gaussian transients and data gaps challenge traditional likelihood-based pipelines, creating an opportunity for simulation-based inference (SBI) to augment traditional analyses. Here, we present an SBI framework for LISA stochastic component separation built on the Simformer, a transformer trained with diffusion or flow matching that ingests the full time-delay interferometry cross-spectral matrix via a physics-aware embedding. We progressively relax standard simplifying assumptions on simulated 11.6-day datasets containing a loud foreground and a faint power-law SGWB. With unequal arms and per-link noise freedom, the networks match 17-dimensional MCMC posteriors after importance reweighting; full cross-spectra constrain each link's optical metrology noise amplitude to $1\%$. When marginalizing over glitches or massive black hole binary transients, estimation errors remain flat up to transient signal-to-noise ratios of several hundred, whereas ignoring them severely biases likelihood fits. Under time-varying spline noise levels, realistic glitch rates, and gaps up to one day long, posterior coverage remains nominal or conservative across $\sim 10^4$ simulations. We test coverage in the local neighborhood of a given observation by introducing a novel importance-weighted localized simulation-based calibration test. We find flow matching slightly better calibrated than diffusion in simpler regimes and observe modest gains from physics-aware template inputs. Finally, we outline how these networks can initialize the noise and stochastic blocks of a LISA global fit.
