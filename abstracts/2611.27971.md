# Soft--hard Bayesian inference in heavy-ion collisions

**Authors:** L. Du, S. A. Bass, M. Chartier, Y. Chen, Y. -C. Chen, R. Datta, R. Dolan, R. Ehlers, H. Elfner, R. J. Fries, C. Gale, D. A. Hangal, B. V. Jacak, P. M. Jacobs, S. Jaiswal, S. Jeon, Y. Ji, S. A. Kirchner, E. Kolbusz, M. Kordell, A. Kumar, R. Kunnawalkam-Elayavalli, J. Latessa, Y. -J. Lee, M. Luzum, A. Majumder, S. Mak, A. Mankolli, C. Martin, H. Mehryar, C. Nattrass, J. Norman, M. Ockleton, J. -F. Paquet, C. Parker, J. H. Putschke, H. Roch, G. Roland, B. Schenke, L. Schwiebert, A. Sengupta, C. Shen, M. Singh, C. Sirimanna, R. A. Soltz, I. Soudi, Y. Tachibana, J. Velkovska, G. Vujanovic, X. Wu, J. Zhang

Bayesian analyses of jet quenching commonly constrain jet--medium interaction parameters using a fixed quark-gluon plasma (QGP) background obtained from a soft-sector calibration. This approach neglects uncertainty in the medium and prevents hard observables from providing additional constraints on its properties. We investigate these effects in Pb--Pb collisions at $\sqrt{s_{NN}}=2.76$ TeV using sequential Bayesian inference in a reduced three-parameter space. We vary the initial normalization and nucleon width of the medium together with the jet--medium coupling, combining JETSCAPE medium evolution with MATTER+LBT calculations of high-$p_T$ hadron and inclusive-jet suppression. We first constrain all three parameters using the hard observables alone, then incorporate a projected soft-sector posterior through sequential inference. We find that the hard observables are sensitive to the medium properties and reveal substantial correlations with the jet--medium coupling, reflecting their compensating effects on jet quenching. Propagating the soft-sector information shifts the preferred coupling relative to a fixed-medium extraction, with the update driven primarily by hadron-suppression measurements. Despite substantial differences in the medium parameters and coupling favored by hard-observable-only and sequential inference, their suppression predictions remain similar over much of the measured range. These findings highlight the difficulty of distinguishing changes in the QGP medium from changes in microscopic jet--medium interaction strength using inclusive suppression measurements alone.
