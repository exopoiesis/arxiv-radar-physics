# Pulsar magnetospheres with stable dissipative current sheets

**Authors:** Ioannis Contopoulos, Spyros Rigas, Ioannis Dimitropoulos

Harris-type current sheets have been studied extensively with particle-in-cell (PIC) simulations that may exhibit unrealistic characteristics when they operate at effective Lundquist numbers far below astrophysical values. Furthermore, localized studies of the pulsar equatorial current sheet generally contain no systematic magnetic-field component normal to the current sheet. Such a component develops naturally in a global pulsar magnetosphere because reconnecting field lines connect the northern and southern magnetic hemispheres. For realistic Lundquist numbers, the normal magnetic field may suppress the tearing instability and control the dissipation in the pulsar equatorial current sheet. We derive the electromagnetic boundary conditions on the surface of the equatorial current sheet imposed by the surrounding ideal force-free magnetosphere and we incorporate them into a global force-free model that we solve with physics-informed neural networks (PINNs). Preliminary solutions indicate two distinct magnetospheric configurations, a nearly ideal CKF-like state with negligible equatorial dissipation, and a strongly dissipative state resembling the solutions of Contopoulos et al. (2014) and Cerutti et al. (2015). These two classes of solutions may help us understand the phenomenon of mode switching in pulsars.
