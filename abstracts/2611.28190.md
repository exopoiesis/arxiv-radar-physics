# Physically Informed Data-Driven Modeling of Low-Pressure Carburizing (LPC): A Self-Calibrating Boundary Condition for Carbon Flux

**Authors:** Alex V. Sagalovych, Tymofii Sagalovych, S. F. Dudnik, Vladislav Sagalovych

This paper formulates an extended framework for a hybrid physically informed data-driven model of low-pressure carburizing (LPC), in which diffusive carbon transport in austenite is described by a physical equation, whereas the effective carbon flux across the gas-metal interface is treated as an unknown boundary forcing to be identified from experimental and process data. The basis for this formulation is that, in LPC, the most uncertain part of the model is not bulk diffusion but the transient surface kinetics of carbon uptake by the metal. In contrast to direct empirical relationships of the 'process regime-result' type, the proposed scheme uses machine learning as a closure operator for the boundary condition in the diffusion problem. This operator is calibrated using carbon concentration profiles, integral mass gain, surface concentration, effective case depth, and additional process measurements. Theoretically, the model is positioned as an operator-learning problem in a system constrained by a partial differential equation, physical flux constraints, inverse-problem regularization, and uncertainty quantification. The relationships between the proposed approach and physics-informed neural networks, operator learning, dynamic-system identification, boundary-operator learning, and boundary-control problems are considered. The mathematical formulation, calibration functional, identifiability conditions, preliminary tuning algorithm, and online adaptation scheme are formulated. The proposed approach is considered as a computational basis for developing interpretable digital support for LPC processes.
