# Random Projection Neural Networks for Phase-Field Models: Model Reformulation, Scaling, and Network Architecture

**Authors:** Chunyan Li, Qi Wang

Phase-field models, particularly the Allen-Cahn(AC) and Cahn-Hilliard(CH) equations, are widely used to describe interfacial dynamics, but resolving their sharp transition layers and stiff dynamics presents substantial numerical challenges. Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) offer a mesh-free approach, yet optimizing large numbers of network parameters can be computationally demanding and may still yield limited accuracy. We develop a random projection neural network (RPNN) framework that combines first-order PDE reformulations with independent random-feature representations of the unknowns. By fixing randomly initialized hidden-layer parameters and optimizing only the output coefficients, RPNNs reduce training to least-squares problems amenable to Newton-type solvers. Introducing auxiliary variables eliminates higher-order derivatives from the PDE residuals, while separate random feature bases in the unknowns provide flexibility in approximating fields with different scales and regularity. For the CH equation, we scale the chemical potential to address the disparity between its magnitude and that of the phase field. We compare the first-order and corresponding higher-order formulations and systematically examine the effects of initialization distributions, the random feature range $R_ξ$, seven activation functions, and dense versus separable space-time bases. We also evaluate gradient-based adaptive sampling, residual reweighting, and mild spatial derivative-penalty regularization. Across the reported benchmarks, the proposed framework reduces relative $L^2$ and absolute $L^\infty$ errors by nearly one to three orders of magnitude compared with the various PINN baselines. These results demonstrate the potential of first-order RPNNs for accurate approximation of phase-field dynamics with sharp transition layers.
