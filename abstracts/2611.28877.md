# Correlation-aware sequential sampling for multi-output polynomial chaos expansions

**Authors:** Qitian Lu, Miroslav Vořechovský, Lukáš Novák

The interaction of multiple physical processes in complex engineering systems is often characterized by several quantities of interest (QoIs) with non-negligible cross-correlations. Accurate uncertainty quantification of these responses requires repeated model evaluations, which can be computationally expensive. As an alternative, polynomial chaos expansion (PCE) provides an efficient surrogate modeling framework for reducing this computational cost owing to its natural structure for statistical analysis, while adaptive sequential sampling can further improve sampling efficiency under a limited model evaluation budget. However, adaptive sampling strategies specifically designed for multi-output PCE remain limited, and existing approaches commonly aggregate information from individual outputs without explicitly accounting for their statistical dependence. When strong correlations exist among QoIs, output-wise aggregation can give greater combined influence to response patterns shared by several outputs. To reduce the influence of such repeated representation on sample allocation, this study develops a principal component analysis-based extension of the multi-output criterion for adaptive construction of PCE. The sampling criterion combines component-wise normalization with the retention of selected principal directions, thereby adjusting the relative influence of shared response patterns while preserving distance-based exploration. The method is verified through numerical examples with different levels of output coupling and is compared with the conventional multi-output criterion and reference sampling strategies. The results demonstrate that accounting for output dependence improves the allocation of new samples among multiple response features and enhances the efficiency of multi-output PCE surrogate construction, particularly for strongly correlated QoIs.
