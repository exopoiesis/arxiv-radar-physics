# Multiscale wave-coupled neural networks for the inverse-design of broadband diffractive optics

**Authors:** Peyton Brown, Riccardo Franchi, Roberto Riganti, Jonathan Schuster, Luca Dal Negro

In this work, we introduce an inverse-design model called multiscale wave-coupled neural networks (MSWNets) inspired by physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) that integrate the deep-learning capability of fully-connected neural networks with rigorous Rayleigh-Sommerfeld diffraction theory. MSWNets are trained using spatial input and output functions describing the height profiles of diffractive structures. Importantly, a MSWNet is composed of multiple sub-networks that provide learning across multiple frequency components of the optimal design solution. First, we validate and analyze the inverse design capabilities of the MSWNets' architecture through training on the complete complex field propagated from the analytical design of an axilens, demonstrating accurate reconstruction. Finally, we employ MSWNets, trained to maximize focusing efficiency, for the inverse design of novel diffractive devices. In particular, we design and display the performance of several achromatic lenses and doublets across the mid-wave infrared region ($3-5\rm{\;μ} m$) with focal distance $f=500\rm{\;μm}$, demonstrating the potential of our inverse-design framework for the next-generation of broadband diffractive optical devices.
