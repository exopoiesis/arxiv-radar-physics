# On extracting Science information from a large data set of RXTE X-ray binary spectra using KDE technique

**Authors:** Ranjeev Misra, Akash Garg, Aman Kumar, Ajit Kembhavi

Machine Learning/Neural Networks have been successively and extensively used in Astronomy (and other subjects) for class identification of objects based on large data sets. However, it remains difficult to extract other science information, such as what the characteristics of the class are that allow for definite identification, and whether there are objects in the sample that are unique and hence of possible science interest. An alternate approach based on rank ordering of variables and Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) may be used for class identification and to infer such science information. We test the method on a previously analyzed data set of RXTE spectra of low-mass X-ray binaries and find that black hole and neutron star systems can be distinguished at an accuracy level of ~93%, which is comparable to deep learning techniques. The soft state spectra of black hole systems are distinct from neutron star ones. For the whole sample, observations of the pulsar 4U 1907+097 (which was inadvertently introduced into the data set) were clearly found to be unique. The previously reported hyper-soft states of GRO J1655-40 and XTE J1550-564 are found to be unique among black hole spectra. For the rest of the neutron stars, the method identifies some unreported spectra of EXO 1745-248 as unique because they seem to be relativistic reflection-dominated spectra. We discuss the potential and scalability of this method.
