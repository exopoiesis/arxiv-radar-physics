# Computable certificates for data-driven dimension reduction on reaction-diffusion lattices

**Authors:** Yuhong Li

Data-driven methods recover low-dimensional coordinates from high-dimensional spatiotemporal data. Whether those coordinates track a genuine invariant object or a finite-resolution artifact is not settled. On reaction-diffusion lattices, the spectral-gap condition of inertial-manifold theory reduces to a critical lattice size $n_*$. Below $n_*$, the two-dimensional diffusion-map reading carries an existence guarantee; above $n_*$ the guarantee lapses, and this lapse does not rule out a slow manifold. The closed form for $n_*$ follows from the reaction Lipschitz constant and the slow-mode growth rate. The certificate is tested on a quadratic and a cubic model system. At the Brusselator boundary the existence sign is checked by interval enclosure, and the spectral margin decays as $n^{-4}$ across the transition. On strongly damped Hopf lattices the slow manifold collapses to the uniform limit cycle; this degeneracy, the trivial graph $\varphi\equiv0$, is reported directly rather than concealed. The boundary gives a PDE-side prior for data-driven reduction: it marks the resolution at which a recovered two-dimensional structure can be trusted.
