# The Science Chapter of the Einstein Telescope Geometry Comparison Report

**Authors:** Alessandra Buonanno, Federico De Lillo, Arnab Dhani, Tim Dietrich, Ulyana Dupletsa, Archisman Ghosh, Ish Gupta, Jan Harms, Francesco Iacovelli, Vuk Mandic, Harsh Narola, B. S. Sathyaprakash, Chris Van den Broeck, Haowen Zhong

This is the Scientific Added Value Chapter of the Einstein Telescope (ET) Geometry Comparison Report, commissioned from the ET Organization by the ET Board of Governmental Representatives. We compare three configurations: a single-site 10-km triangle ($Δ$) and two 15-km L-shaped detectors (2L) at widely separated sites, either nearly aligned or misaligned. We use metrics representative of the ET science case: compact-binary populations, dense matter, cosmography, stochastic backgrounds, multimessenger astronomy, fundamental physics, and continuous and burst sources. All three configurations deliver the ET science case, but they excel in different areas. For most sensitivity-driven science, the 2L configurations outperform the $Δ$. The misaligned 2L is best for localizing and characterizing individual sources, multimessenger astronomy, and bright-siren cosmology. The nearly aligned 2L is best for stochastic backgrounds. The $Δ$ localizes sources better than the aligned 2L and performs comparably to both 2L configurations for continuous and burst sources and at kilohertz frequencies. A 2L orientation of $30^\circ$ is a good compromise between stochastic-background sensitivity and localization. We validate Fisher information matrix (FIM) forecasts against Bayesian inference for $\sim550$ binary black hole and neutron star-black hole signals. Despite substantial differences, the FIM shows no systematic preference for either configuration for intrinsic parameters. For distance and sky localization, the FIM cannot capture multimodality and long-range correlations, leading to larger discrepancies, which however do not affect the ranking. Adding Cosmic Explorer to the network improves the reach of all three ET configurations, especially in volume localization and characterization of high-redshift sources. Differences between geometries shrink, but the ranking is largely unchanged.
