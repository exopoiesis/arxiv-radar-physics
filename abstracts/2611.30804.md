# Random Feature Neural Networks for Stochastic Optimal Control

**Authors:** Guangyi He, Damir Filipovic, Lukas Gonon

Random feature neural networks (RFNNs) sample their hidden parameters once and train only the output layer. We analyze RFNNs as feedback policies for finite-horizon stochastic control with trajectory-dependent objectives. A policy change alters later states, so the error that matters is the objective error of the induced trajectory rather than a pointwise regression error. We extend the quantitative random-feature approximation theory for PDE solutions and nonlinear regression to feedback control. We investigate the objective error of an RFNN policy through two sources, approximation of the policy by random features and estimation of the objective from finitely many paths. A stability estimate for the controlled trajectory turns a policy error into an objective error. Under bounded actions, a regular benchmark policy and exact empirical minimization, the expected objective gap to the benchmark policy is at most of order $N^{-1/2}+\sqrt{N(1+\log N)/n}$, with $N$ the feature width per output coordinate and $n$ the number of paths. At fixed sample size the two terms trade off in $N$, and balancing them gives $N\asymp\sqrt{n/\log n}$. Across a wide range of examples, estimated gaps generally decrease with width and sample size, and RFNNs show an advantage when simulated paths are limited. We also study Parsimonious Random Feature Learning (PRFL), which grows and prunes the random dictionary during training while training only the output coefficients. At matched retained width and training budget, PRFL improves on a fixed RFNN in the LQ and Heston comparisons, while the fully trained network remains best in those examples.
