# Distillation of a Normalising Flow into a Compact Symbolic Expression for Real-Time Anomaly Detection at the LHC Level-1 Trigger

**Authors:** Justin Bollhalder, Florencia Canelli, Valentina Guglielmi, Roope Oskari Niemi, Maurizio Pierini, Chang Sun, Francesco Vaselli

At the CERN Large Hadron Collider, anomaly detection techniques are becoming popular alternatives to traditional signature-aware new physics searches. For this approach to avoid any bias induced by the real-time event selection applied in the trigger system of ATLAS and CMS, it should be implemented before the first stage of selection, the Level-1 Trigger. This requires deciding to accept or reject each collision within a few microseconds, a latency budget that excludes the most expressive anomaly-detection models. Knowledge distillation has successfully addressed this issue by replacing a complex anomaly-detection model with a lighter student network. This work takes the compression one step further, distilling an anomaly detection model based on a normalising flow into an explicit mathematical formula. Using the SymbolNet symbolic-regression framework with adaptive pruning, the student is a learned functional form based on a chosen subset of the inputs, providing at once feature selection and compression through knowledge distillation. The final expression contains 577 operations and matches the teacher's discrimination on a test dataset, generalising to four benchmark new physics scenarios. Implemented on a Xilinx Virtex UltraScale+ VU13P FPGA, the symbolic student has a nine-cycle latency, corresponding to 42.8\,ns at the post-route maximum clock frequency, and uses 0.81\% of the device's LUTs and 0.44\% of its DSP slices.
