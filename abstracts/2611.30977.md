# A data-driven absolute amplitude calibration for the Radio Neutrino Observatory Greenland (RNO-G)

The Radio Neutrino Observatory Greenland (RNO-G) aims to detect ultra-high-energy neutrinos (E $\geq 100\,$PeV) through the nanosecond-long coherent radio pulses produced by the Askaryan effect when neutrino-induced particle cascades develop in the Greenlandic ice sheet. Precise calibration of the analog signal chain of each antenna channel is essential for accurate reconstruction of neutrino-induced events and their physical properties. In this work, we present a data-driven calibration method based on measurement of the ambient radio background. By combining first-principles simulations of the physical noise contributions with a parametric model of the detector response, we determine the absolute gain of each antenna channel. The resulting calibration provides a season-averaged gain for every channel with an accuracy of approximately 0.5\,dB (better than 5\%), while the investigated systematic uncertainties associated with the noise signal-model were found to be negligible. This work represents the first application of an ambient-noise-based calibration that utilizes a multi-component model for the ambient noise, originally developed for radio air-shower experiments, to an in-ice radio neutrino detector.
