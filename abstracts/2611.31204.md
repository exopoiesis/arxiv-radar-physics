# JAISP: A Joint Rubin-Euclid Foundation Model for Cross-Instrument Source Detection and Astrometry

**Authors:** Shoubaneh Hemmati, George Helou, Yu-Heng Lin, Rachel Akeson, Charles Darren Dowell, Andreas Faisst, Thomas Greene, David Shupe

Joint pixel-level processing of Rubin and Euclid imaging promises deeper source detection and the cross-survey consistency that precision cosmology requires. We introduce JAISP (Joint AI Survey Processing), a foundation model that learns a single representation of ten-band imaging: Rubin ugrizy and Euclid VIS, Y, J, and H, from the first public releases over $\approx 0.2$ deg$^2$ of the ECDFS deep field. Trained without labels by predicting each band from the other nine, its frozen encoder drives small task-specific networks for source detection and astrometry. The detection network recovers nearly all VIS-selected Euclid Q1 catalog sources on held-out sky, and source injection shows that combining all ten bands extends the point-source detection limit by $\approx 0.3$ mag beyond VIS alone. The astrometry network transfers the sharp VIS localization to the other nine bands, reducing median cross-band positional offsets from $\approx 50$ to $6$--$10$ mas while retaining the full precision of its VIS centroid anchor even for faint sources. Separating this per-source scatter exposes the cross-survey concordance field: a coherent $9$--$10$ mas offset between the two independently Gaia-registered astrometric solutions, dominated by a component common to all bands. Applied without retraining to a second Euclid Deep Field, both networks perform without measurable loss. These gains serve the joint-survey science case: a deeper census recovers faint neighbors whose blending biases weak-lensing shapes and photometric redshifts, and VIS-grade positions in every band supply the common astrometric reference that joint photometry and faint-source proper motions require. A single representation learned once from survey pixels thus serves several measurements across the sky.
