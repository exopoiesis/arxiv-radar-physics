# Neural-operator acceleration of bidirectional phonon Boltzmann-Fourier coupling for multiscale heat transport in silicon fin arrays

**Authors:** Ruthwik Chivukula, Khemraj Shukla, George Em Karniadakis

Localized heating in semiconductor devices can produce non-diffusive phonon transport near heat-generating regions, while heat spreading through the surrounding substrate remains well described by Fourier conduction. We present a hybrid framework that couples a pretrained neural operator for the phonon Boltzmann transport equation (BTE) within individual fins to a Fourier model of a shared substrate. To reduce the cost of repeated BTE solves across fin positions and coupling iterations, a DeepONet maps each fin's substrate-interface temperature and heating state to its temperature and heat-flux fields. The operator is trained on interface temperatures generated by a linearized proxy of the coupled problem, which avoids explicit training data collection from full coupled runs. An energy-balance penalty regularizes the learned operator, and a mixed finite-element formulation represents substrate heat flux in an H(div)-conforming space. The two models exchange temperature and normal heat flux through a non-overlapping Schwarz iteration with adaptive Aitken relaxation. We evaluate the framework on an idealized three-dimensional array of 100 silicon fins. For a held-out switching configuration, the neural-operator-Fourier solution differs from the direct BTE-Fourier reference by a maximum of 0.3 K in temperature and 0.6% in relative flux across all fins. Excluding the one-time substrate factorization, the coupled surrogate run takes about 50 s, compared with 2 h 28 min for direct coupling, a speedup of approximately 180x. Additional held-out switching patterns converge with the same trained operator. These results demonstrate neural-operator acceleration of bidirectional kinetic-continuum coupling for the tested geometry and material. This bidirectional strategy may extend to other multiscale materials problems coupling learned local neural models with continuum solvers.
