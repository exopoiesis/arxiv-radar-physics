# SPARK: Spectral Preconditioned Analytic Residual Kolmogorov Arnold Networks for incompressible Navier Stokes equations

**Authors:** Dathi Rajasekhar, Thien-Tam Nguyen, Trung Bao Le

We propose Spectral Jet KAN (SJ-KAN) to reduce the cost of differentiation and the network complexity of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) for the Navier-Stokes (NS) equations. The input coordinates are embedded through Chebyshev bases whose spatial derivatives are known in closed form, and all differentiation matrices are computed once for the collocation points before training. A single linear layer compresses the Chebyshev bases into a few latent features, and a single nonlinear Chebyshev Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (CKAN) layer maps these features to the physical variables. Because the basis derivatives are known in advance and the compression is linear, the spatial derivatives in the NS equations are carried through the network analytically by a fixed sequence of matrix products and elementwise operations. Automatic differentiation (AD) is used only in backpropagation, to compute the gradients of the loss with respect to the network weights. We examine three variants of SJ-KAN (JetKAN, JetKAN-F, JetKAN-H), which differ in how the output layer maps the latent features to the physical variables. All variants are tested on the Taylor-Green vortex and on the lid driven cavity at Reynolds numbers Re = 100, 1000, 5000 and 10,000. The results are compared with a KAN that uses AD (PIKAN) and with a standard PINN. The SJ-KAN variants reach comparable or better accuracy than both, with up to 47 times fewer parameters and up to 8.2 times faster training per epoch. SJ-KAN also solves the lid driven cavity flow at Re = 10,000 without any reference data and with fewer than 9,000 trainable parameters. These results show that analytic derivative propagation, a small spectral input layer and a single KAN layer give a faster and simpler solver than methods based on AD, also for flows at high Reynolds numbers.
