# Physics-informed neural networks for strong normal shocks in kinetic relaxation models and the hard-sphere Boltzmann equation

**Authors:** Ehsan Roohi

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) for kinetic equations have mostly been applied near equilibrium and judged by density, velocity and temperature. Here a network trained on the kinetic equation alone, with no reference data in the loss, solves steady normal shock waves and is assessed in eight fields: density, velocity, temperature, heat flux, stress, a third-order moment and two fourth-order moments. The network corrects a mixture of the two end-state Maxwellians by an unbounded, signed factor with exact inflow; the loss, the residual in three norms with conservation terms, is minimised by Adam and a self-scaled quasi-Newton method with continuation in the Mach number. For the hard-sphere Boltzmann equation at Mach 8, in a comparison made once after reference-free tests, the largest relative $L^2$ difference from direct simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) over the eight fields is 0.19 per cent; at Mach 10 it is 0.34 per cent. The distribution function itself agrees with the reference over six decades in velocity space. For the Bhatnagar--Gross--Krook (BGK) model, a blind prediction at Mach 50 gives density, velocity and temperature to about 0.1 per cent and exposes a loss of accuracy confined to the two fourth-order moments. Its origin is identified as a passive reduced distribution that carries little weight in the loss; with a larger weight on that distribution and a longer optimisation, both moments are within 3.2 per cent of the discrete-velocity solution up to Mach 70 and 7.1 per cent at Mach 100. The Shakhov model is solved to Mach 20 and a Rykov-type model of nitrogen to Mach 15.
