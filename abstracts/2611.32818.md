# Data-driven Reduced-order Modeling of a Multistable Viscoelastic Flow

**Authors:** Shaoxuan Chen, Su Yang, Simon J. Haward, Amy Q. Shen, Panayotis G. Kevrekidis

Viscoelastic flow past two side-by-side microcylinders has been shown to exhibit geometry-dependent transitions among symmetric, converging, diverging, and asymmetric-diverging flow states, together with regions of bistability and tristability. Here, we develop a data-driven framework for learning a two-dimensional, parameter-dependent normal form for this bifurcation structure from sparse measurements of stable flow states. We use the flow asymmetry parameters $I'$ and $I''$ as state variables, with the gap ratio $G$ and Weissenberg number $Wi$ as control parameters. In the absence of trajectory data, the measured steady states are enforced as stable equilibria of the learned vector field through equilibrium and local-stability constraints. We examine both polynomial and neural-network representations of the associated normal form and incorporate the physical reflection symmetry directly into the model. The resulting models recover the main geometry-dependent transitions and the overall structure of the experimental phase diagram. To reduce additional stable equilibria that are not constrained by the measurements, we introduce an iterative negative-sample procedure that refines the predicted equilibrium structure. Combining equilibrium data, local stability, physical symmetry, and negative samples yields the closest agreement with the experimental phase diagram among the models considered. The remaining discrepancies reflect the limited information available, solely in the form of sparse equilibrium measurements. Overall, the framework aims to showcase a prototypical example of both the potential and the limitations of inferring nonlinear flow dynamics from sparse equilibrium measurements and highlight the importance of physical constraints and additional dynamical information for reconstructing complex bifurcation structures.
