# Theory-Informed Quantum Error Mitigation for Shor Order Finding with Fourier Neural Operators

**Authors:** Stefano Markidis, Qingxin Yang, Erik Åsgrim, Ivy Peng

Quantum noise distorts the distribution of circuit measurement outcomes, often leaving enough information to infer the algorithm's correct result. To recover this latent information from the noisy distribution in Shor order finding, we introduce a theory-informed quantum error mitigation method. We model how circuit errors shift, broaden, or weaken peaks in the output distribution. These models guide both the generation of synthetic training data and the design of a Fourier neural operator with a learned gain that attenuates or amplifies higher retained Fourier modes. We score each candidate order by averaging the correlations between its ideal distribution and the distributions measured in three circuit executions. The network adjusts these scores to guide the order estimate and determine the weights of a normalized mixture of the ideal distributions. On 1,351 simulated test cases for general modular exponentiation, held out by circuit configuration, exact order recovery rises from 19% to 34%, and the median Jensen-Shannon divergence from the ideal distribution falls by 50%, from 0.64 to 0.32. On 101 Mersenne rotation circuits executed across five IBM quantum processors, a separate model trained and calibrated in simulation increases the number of correct order estimates from 35 to 53. Algorithmic structure can remain recoverable even when noise makes it barely visible.
