# From dispersal to patrol: the extracellular matrix shapes an annealing-like search by Drosophila embryonic macrophages

**Authors:** Nickolay Korabel, Tom H. Millard, Thomas Andrew Waigh

Hemocytes, the macrophages of Drosophila, disperse through the embryo and then patrol its tissues, moving along basement membranes that they help to build. Because the persistence (H) and amplitude (D) of hemocyte motion vary along each track, it is difficult to describe how movement changes during development and how it depends on basement membrane composition. We analysed trajectories, obtained by selective plane illumination microscopy, of hemocytes in control embryos and in embryos deficient in the basement membrane components laminin (LanB1-deficient) or collagen IV (collagen-deficient). A physics-informed neural network estimates H and D in short windows, each with a calibrated uncertainty. From them we construct an effective diffusivity Deff, defined for any anomalous diffusion as the diffusivity of the unpredictable part of the motion. Its error is uncorrelated with that of H and acts as an effective temperature. Cells decelerate during development by cooling (falling Deff) and forgetting (falling H). Control cells cool gradually while staying persistent. LanB1-deficient cells cool further, forget, lose the coupling between speed and persistence and become nearly Brownian. Collagen-deficient cells cool but keep most of their persistence. Hemocyte movement thus resembles an annealed search, in which laminin, rather than collagen IV, keeps patrolling cells persistent.
