# Towards three-dimensional dark matter mapping in the extended solar neighbourhood with Gaia Data Release 3 and deep learning

**Authors:** Yigon Kim, Ho Seong Hwang, Sungwook E. Hong, Donghui Jeong, Mingyeong Yang, Ji-hoon Kim

We investigate a method for inferring the local dark matter (DM) density in the solar neighbourhood using a deep learning framework that learns the mapping between stellar kinematics and the underlying DM density field from cosmological simulations. To this end, we construct a training set from 7 Milky Way-like galaxies in the FIRE-2 simulation suite. We use three-dimensional stellar kinematic components, consisting of the tangential velocities along the right ascension and declination directions ($v_{α*}$ and $v_δ$) derived from parallaxes and proper motions, together with radial velocity, as input features to predict the three-dimensional DM density field. Two distinct architectures, VNet (arXiv:1606.04797) and ViT-VNet (arXiv:2205.08534), are trained on identical datasets. Both models reproduce the large-scale DM density gradient, whereas small-scale fluctuations are systematically smoothed. Applying the trained models to Gaia DR3 data yields our best estimates of the local DM density: $0.80^{+0.10}_{-0.10}$ and $1.12^{+0.15}_{-0.13}$ GeV cm$^{-3}$ from VNet and ViT-VNet, respectively. The quoted uncertainties reflect the inference scatter across the training set. We further examine systematics from model dependence and the simulation-Gaia domain mismatch, providing an initial step towards a framework for reconstructing local DM maps from future multi-epoch astrometric measurements, including those expected from Gaia DR4.
