# Data-driven framework for invariant manifold identification in multiscale systems

**Authors:** Dimitris M. Manias, Ismaila Muhammed, Dimitris A. Goussis, Haralampos Hatzikirou

Multiscale dynamical systems often exhibit fast transient behavior followed by slow evolution on a low-dimensional attracting manifold, on which their long-term dynamics unfold. Identifying this structure directly from data remains challenging when the governing equations, Jacobians, and fast-slow variable partitions are unknown. In this work, we propose a fully data-driven framework for timescale decomposition, slow invariant manifold (SIM) approximation, and reduced-order model identification. A sparse-regression surrogate of the vector field is reconstructed from the data, and its Jacobian, estimated with a matrix-free Krylov method, is analysed with Computational Singular Perturbation diagnostics. The resulting fast-slow partition guides the learning of explicit SIM algebraic representations through regression, and the reduced model follows by restricting the dynamics to it. For a linear stochastic system, this regression agrees with the Mori-Zwanzig projection at leading order in the timescale ratio. We demonstrate the validity of the framework on an illustrative linear system, the Michaelis-Menten model, a biofilm growth model, and a five-dimensional target-mediated drug disposition (TMDD) model. The method accurately identifies fast variables, reconstructs slow invariant manifolds, and generates accurate reduced models. Furthermore, we map the performance boundaries of the framework under sparse sampling and measurement noise, showing robust recovery for low-dimensional systems and highlighting data-requirement limits in higher dimensions. These results demonstrate that interpretable multiscale reduction can be achieved directly from time-series data, without requiring explicit prior knowledge of the underlying fast/slow timescale separation.
