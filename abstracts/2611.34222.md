# The Socratic PAUSE: safeguarding scientific judgement at AI speed

**Authors:** Abdulkadir Celik, Sir Bashir M. Al-Hashimi

Generative AI can turn a tentative scientific question into hypotheses, methods, code and interpretations within minutes. Yet generating a contribution does not establish that it deserves scientific reliance: researchers must still examine its evidence, assumptions and implications, and remain accountable for the decision to rely on it. Using Einstein's sketch of scientific discovery as a lens, we argue that AI has accelerated discovery far more than justification. In human-AI research partnerships, confidence can therefore outgrow evidence through three mechanisms: recursive reinforcement, loss of provenance and miscalibrated reliance on AI-generated contributions. Verification debt accumulates when such contributions are carried into later claims or decisions without adequate scrutiny. Accordingly, we propose the Socratic Pause as a deliberate checkpoint at consequential decision gates, where an AI contribution remains provisional while a researcher examines its basis. The PAUSE protocol structures this examination through five steps: Pin down the claim, Ask and challenge, Uncover assumptions and alternatives, Scrutinise the evidence and Exercise judgement. It concludes with a recorded decision to accept, revise, reject or defer the contribution. We show how AI research tools can support this practice without displacing human judgement, and outline a framework for evaluating whether it improves verification, decision-making and independent understanding. The aim is to preserve the freedom to explore with AI while ensuring that those responsible for the work can say what was examined, what remains uncertain and why the next step is justified.
