# LatENSO v1.0: a learned latent state of the tropical Pacific for seasonal ENSO operators and forecasting

**Authors:** Milad Panahi, Annalisa Bracco

Here we present LatENSO v1.0, a machine-learning forecast system for the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) built around a learned, 32-dimensional state of the tropical Pacific. A Swin-transformer autoencoder compresses thirteen monthly-averaged ocean and atmosphere fields into 32 numbers, trained to reconstruct the fields, with no forecast target. The 32 numbers rebuild the fields as well as O(100) empirical orthogonal functions. Two seasonal operators use this state. The first is the extended nonlinear recharge oscillator (XRO) with two directions of the learned state among its variables; we call it XRO-lat. The second is a Floquet modal operator (FMO) of three damped oscillators, which forecasts a correction added to a least-squares base forecast made from four observed predictors. We evaluate both against the recharge oscillator, XRO and a deep echo-state network (DESN), all refitted on identical data, cross-validation folds and verification windows (1981-2024). Over lead times of 1-18 months, XRO-lat reaches an anomaly correlation coefficient (ACC) of 0.673, the highest of all tropical-Pacific-only (in-domain) systems and statistically indistinguishable from XRO and DESN with their eight remote-basin indices (0.685 and 0.667). From lead 15 to lead 24 it is the most skillful of all systems. Over leads 1-18, the FMO forecast (0.619) is statistically indistinguishable from every in-domain reference model (0.631-0.654). What the FMO adds to its base forecast, 0.054 in ACC, is carried by its Floquet propagation, whose generator consistently holds a prominent quasi-biennial oscillator. At leads 3-9 the FMO trails XRO, DESN and the in-domain XRO, a deficit partly attributable to its base. LatENSO combines competitive skill with trustworthiness: a compact state whose physical content can be inspected, and fully open access to code, weights and data.
