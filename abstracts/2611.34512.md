# A Genetic Algorithm Approach to Reconstructing Scalar-Tensor Lagrangians

**Authors:** Mikel Artola, George Alestas, Dimitrios Efstratiou, Evangelos Achilleas Paraskevas

Recent DESI baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) data, combined with cosmic microwave background (CMB) and Type Ia supernovae observations, have been interpreted as evidence for dynamical dark energy whose equation of state crosses the phantom divide $w=-1$. Such a crossing cannot be realized smoothly by a minimally coupled scalar field $\varphi$, but can arise naturally in scalar-tensor theories. We present a data-driven framework that reconstructs the scalar-tensor Lagrangian directly from observations, using a genetic algorithm to evolve the symbolic expressions for the nonminimal coupling $F(\varphi)$ and the scalar potential $U(\varphi)$. Each candidate is evaluated through a forward model that self-consistently determines the expansion history, the effective gravitational coupling, and the growth of structure. Candidates are ranked by a joint likelihood of DESI DR2 BAO, Pantheon+ supernovae calibrated with SH0ES Cepheids, compressed CMB distance priors, and redshift-space distortion measurements of $fσ_8$. We restrict to the unscreened branch of the theory and impose Solar System constraints on the post-Newtonian parameter $γ$ and on the time variation of the gravitational coupling, together with a Big Bang nucleosynthesis bound on its evolution. Across 64 independent runs, the best reconstruction improves the fit relative to $Λ$CDM by $Δχ^2=-16.6$, compared with $Δχ^2=-10.6$ for the CPL dark-energy parametrization. The preferred coupling is well described by $F(\varphi)=c_1\varphi^2+e^{\varphi}(1-c_2\varphi)$. The reconstructed theory shows phantom behavior at $z\gtrsim0.5$, but it remains close to $w=-1$ at low redshift.
