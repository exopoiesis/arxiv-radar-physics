Inverse Reconstruction of Causal Nonlinear Electrodynamics: Exact Functional Families and Single-Horizon Black-Hole Solutions

Ariel Guzmán, Mohsen Fathi, J. R. Villanueva

arXiv:2609.16016·gr-qc·Published 2026-09-03

We formulate nonlinear electrodynamics as a constitutive inverse problem: integrability, the Maxwell limit, and causal propagation are imposed before a Lagrangian is chosen. Within an affine-separable reduction of the two-invariant problem, we obtain an infinite-dimensional causal class whose functional freedom is encoded by a bounded logarithmic index. The class is generically non-self-dual; Born-Infeld is its unique self-dual member, and it contains closed-form birefringent theories beyond the known power-law line. On the magnetic axis, complete monotonicity yields a positive spectral representation. Finite monopole self-energy is equivalent to a spectral moment of order $-1/4$. Global magnetic causality forces the spectral support to accumulate at the origin, while finite self-energy excludes an atom there; together these conditions rule out finite atomic measures. For generalized-gamma spectra, global magnetic causality and finite self-energy select the exact window $1/4<γ\leq 1/2$ for every shape parameter. We further determine when these magnetic laws admit a causal two-invariant completion and give an explicit spectral realization of a non-power member of the full class. Coupling the reconstructed matter to Einstein gravity produces exact geometries and a monotonicity criterion that allows at most one positive horizon. Finally, the two optical metrics of the full class are obtained in closed form and shown to possess intersecting causal cones, establishing symmetric hyperbolicity under the stated regularity assumptions.
