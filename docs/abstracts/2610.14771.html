A QoE-Driven Framework for O-RAN Network Slicing Based on Empirical Multi-User VR Traffic Modelling

Ahmed Chyad, David Naseh, Maryam Hafeez, Syed Ali Raza Zaidi

arXiv:2610.14771·eess.SP·Published 2026-10-10

Virtual Reality (VR) requires strict Quality-of-Experience (QoE) guarantees, making radio resource management harder. Static slice allocations are wasteful, while dynamic approaches often rely on heuristic penalty functions lacking an empirical basis. We introduce a QoE-driven network-slicing framework for Open RAN in which resource allocation is guided by empirically derived multi-user VR traffic statistics. Closed-form n-fold convolutions model the aggregate data rate and inter-arrival time of n concurrent users, validated empirically for up to four. The fitted data-rate cumulative distribution function is checked against the measured throughput to give a percentile-based satisfaction indicator; the inter-arrival law sets the slice targets. A near-real-time RIC xApp closes a loop over the E2SM-KPM and E2SM-RC interfaces to apply slice-level Physical Resource Block (PRB) quotas. On an over-the-air testbed it reclaims up to 41% of the cell's committed PRBs from a greedy neighbouring slice while never reducing the protected VR slice. An analytical study then estimates the PRB demand and per-user QoE across user counts and channel qualities, giving the admission boundary beyond which the target QoE is unattainable. A controlled comparison further shows that a fixed-rate synthetic load overstates the VR slice's PRB footprint by 68%, showing the necessity of data-driven slice dimensioning.
