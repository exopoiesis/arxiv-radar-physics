Aspects of $\mathcal{N}=2$ SUSY gauge theories and applications to Quantum Field Theory and Gravity

Andrea Cipriani

arXiv:2610.15619·hep-th·Published 2026-10-11

This thesis investigates the application of techniques developed in supersymmetric gauge theories to physical problems in Quantum Field Theory and Gravity, focusing on the Seiberg-Witten (SW) curve and its quantum version. After reviewing the essential concepts of Supersymmetry and the derivation of the SW curve, we explore its applications in both fields. In Quantum Field Theory, we investigate the emergence of Argyres-Douglas theories in the moduli space of supersymmetric models, focusing on extremal correlators. We analyze a conformal model to compute their instanton corrections, finding agreement with predictions from Effective Field Theory, and discuss the implications for analogous observables in Argyres-Douglas theories. In Gravity, we exploit the quantum SW curve and its relation to differential equations describing physical systems. We establish the linear stability of Topological Stars, arising in the fuzzball proposal, under massive charged scalar perturbations. We also investigate charge superradiance in configurations with a horizon and derive a novel hypergeometric representation of the confluent Heun function, relevant to gravitational-wave propagation in the Schwarzschild background. This result yields a new resummed expression for waveform tail contributions, in excellent agreement with numerical data. Finally, beyond the SW formalism, we develop a Machine Learning algorithm based on Hamiltonian Neural Networks to reproduce the geodesics of a neutral particle in a fuzzball geometry. For critical trajectories, our method outperforms standard numerical integrators in both efficiency and accuracy.
