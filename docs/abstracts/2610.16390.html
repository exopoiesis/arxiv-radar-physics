QBFF: Deep Quantum Bottleneck for Automatic Speaker Verification Anti-Spoofing

Anton Firc, Nouhaila Innan, Vojtěch Staněk, Kamil Malinka, Martin Perešini, Zbyněk Lička, Walid El Maouaki, Alberto Marchisio, Muhammad Shafique

arXiv:2610.16390·quant-ph·Published 2026-10-12

A central goal of quantum machine learning is to identify applications in which small quantum circuits can improve task performance while remaining executable on real hardware. We investigate deepfake (synthetic) speech detection, a largely unexplored application in this area, where reliable confidence scores are as important as discrimination performance because deployment decisions are made at fixed thresholds and overconfident errors can be costly. We introduce QBFF, which routes a compressed speech representation through a small 8-qubit variational circuit (VQC) used as a drop-in bottleneck, with a matched classical bottleneck as the control. The quantum circuit yields better-calibrated confidence in most matched comparisons, including out-of-distribution evaluation, though the downstream deployment-cost benefit is model-dependent. We then run the same circuit on a real IBM quantum processor. To our knowledge, this is the first anti-spoofing pipeline to run on a QPU; hardware execution demonstrates feasibility, with lower subset EER and $C_{llr}$ but higher mean actDCF than the matched classical reference. Together, these results show that hardware-executable quantum bottlenecks can improve score reliability, with gains that vary by evaluation setting.
