Establishing baseline model performances for optical turbulence forecasting

M. De Sepibus, E. Masciadri, C. Weinberger, A. Otarola, S. Miesske, C. Veillet, S. Ragland

arXiv:2610.18702·astro-ph.IM·Published 2026-10-13

Accurate forecasting of optical turbulence (OT) is useful to optimise telescope scheduling and maximising observing efficiency, as the performance of adaptive optics systems strongly depends on turbulence conditions. While various numerical weather prediction and machine learning models are developed to predict these complex turbulence properties, properly evaluating their added value requires well-defined baseline reference models. This study establishes baseline performance thresholds for key astroclimatic parameters using two distinct models: one forecasting the parameter average over a specific timescale, and the other forecasting the temporal evolution over the same specific timescale. We apply these frameworks to extensive, multi-year datasets from the sites of the Very Large Telescope (VLT) and the Large Binocular Telescope (LBT) and discuss the possibility of extrapolating reference values of forecast performances for other sites based on their optical turbulence statistical characteristics. Our results show that baseline predictive accuracy varies according to the observing site, the specific parameter, the chosen timescale, and the forecasting approach. Therefore, an OT forecasting model's accuracy cannot be quantified in absolute terms, but relatively to the baseline.
