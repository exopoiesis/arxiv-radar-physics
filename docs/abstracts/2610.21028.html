ACACIAS II: element abundances for 39 stars in the dwarf galaxy Sculptor

Anell Cornejo-Cárdenas, Sven Buder, Melissa K. Ness, J. Trevor Mendel

arXiv:2610.21028·astro-ph.GA·Published 2026-10-14

Element abundances in nearby galaxies provide insights into their chemical enrichment histories and place the Milky Way and its neighbours in a broader cosmological context. These systems can be studied through individual stars, whose resolved spectra enable the construction of a chemical abundance census of the Local Group. We present the second stage of the ACACIAS (extra-galactic ArChAeology with Cannon Inferred Abundances from resolved integral field unit Spectra) program, which aims to build the first homogeneous data set of stellar abundances across dwarf galaxies spanning a range of masses for comparison with Milky Way properties. We analyse low-resolution (R=1800), low-SNR (signal-to-noise ratio, ~ 25) MUSE spectra for 39 stars in the Sculptor dwarf spheroidal galaxy. Using the data-driven method The Cannon, we combine high-fidelity labels from the Apache Point Observatory Galactic Evolution Experiment (APOGEE) survey with MUSE-like Large Sky Area Multi-Object Fiber Spectroscopic Telescope (LAMOST) spectra to analyse the MUSE data. This enables the determination of [Mg/Fe], [Mn/Fe], [C/Fe], [N/Fe], and [Al/Fe] on the APOGEE abundance scale, with precisions of σ = 0.09-0.37 dex. Comparing model configurations, we show that [Mg/Fe], [Mn/Fe], and [C/Fe] are best recovered from targeted spectral windows, whereas stellar parameters and [Al/Fe] benefit from full-spectrum information with telluric masking. This reflects the complementary strengths of restricted-window models, which recover abundances from direct spectral features, and full-spectrum models, which exploit correlations with other labels when diagnostic features are weak or absent. Our approach demonstrates the potential of low-resolution, low-SNR stellar spectra to build homogeneous datasets for extragalactic archaeology to better understand the chemical enrichment diversity of the Local Group.
