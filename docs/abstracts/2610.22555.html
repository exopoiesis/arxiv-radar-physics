Atmospheric Chemistry-Climate Modeling with an AI Foundation Model

James Weber, Makoto Kelp

arXiv:2610.22555·physics.ao-ph·Published 2026-10-15

Atmospheric composition and chemistry-climate interactions are central to predicting future climate change. Yet simulating chemical and aerosol processes is typically the most expensive component of an atmospheric model and is often highly simplified or prescribed entirely. AI weather forecasting foundation models have shown skill rivalling process-based models and are increasingly fine-tuned for new atmospheric tasks. Here, we present the first foundation model to capture interactions between emissions, atmospheric composition, and radiation. We fine-tune the Aurora Earth system foundation model on simulations from the chemistry-climate model UKCA. Our model reproduces daily meteorology, composition, aerosol, and radiation fields of the coupled system with 23 of 27 predicted fields achieving $R^2$ above 0.85 in both the troposphere and stratosphere. It reproduces ozone, CO, and temperature with annual mean biases below 4% and the downwelling shortwave and longwave fluxes to within 6%. Our model remains stable in autoregressive simulation over 50 years while running ~$100\times$ faster than UKCA. By training on paired emissions scenarios, our model learns responses to perturbations by capturing tropospheric ozone's response to changes in NO and CO emissions and surface radiation's response to aerosol. We further show that incorporating MLS satellite ozone retrievals into training corrects biases in the underlying UKCA fields, reducing zonal mean ozone error against collocated observations by 23% between 250 and 20 hPa. Our approach does not suggest process-based modeling can be replaced but demonstrates that a foundation model can be adapted to respond to forcings and learn from observations at a relatively small computational cost.
