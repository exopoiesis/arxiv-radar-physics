Black-Hole Ringdown from Photon-Sphere Modes in an Elastic Analogue

Jiaoqing Wang, Chenni Xu, Sophia Purow, Sébastien Guenneau, Patrick Sebbah

arXiv:2610.30396·physics.optics·Published 2026-10-21

Quasinormal modes (QNMs) govern the ringdown of black holes, yet their physical origin and experimental accessibility remain limited. Here we create an elastic analogue of a black hole using a curved metallic shell engineered to emulate a Schwarzschild-like metric, and directly probe its vibrational dynamics in the time domain. Following impulsive excitation, the elastic system exhibits a clear ringdown response, providing a tangible analogue of black-hole ringing. The underlying modes are flexural vibrations localized near the photon sphere. A reduction of the thin-shell elastic equations yields a photon-sphere effective potential and predicts the dependence of the localized-mode frequencies on azimuthal order. Crucially, they persist even when boundary reflections are strongly suppressed, demonstrating that their confinement is governed by a curvature-induced effective potential rather than by cavity boundaries. This platform enables direct access to quasinormal modes through both their temporal decay and spectral signatures, establishing a direct connection between photon-sphere dynamics and ringdown. More broadly, our results identify curvature-induced trapping as a general mechanism for wave localization and dissipation in open systems.
