Deep-Learning Identification and Global Characterization of Energetic O2+ Beams at Mars

Chi Zhang, Chuanfei Dong, Kathleen G. Hanley, Yaxue Dong, Hans Nilsson, Eduard Dubinin, Jasper Halekas, Janet Luhmann, Shannon Curry, Jiawei Gao, Yilan Qin, Xinmin Li, Hongyang Zhou, Liang Wang

arXiv:2610.30578·physics.space-ph·Published 2026-10-22

Martian ionospheric ions can be accelerated away from Mars by the solar wind, contributing substantially to atmospheric ion escape. Here, we develop a U-Net-based image segmentation model to identify a distinct population of escaping planetary ions, energetic O2+ beams, from a decade of Mars Atmosphere and Volatile EvolutioN (MAVEN) observations and perform an event-based statistical analysis of their global properties and dependence on external conditions. We find that O2+ beams are widely distributed throughout the Martian plasma environment, with a strong preference for the +E hemisphere, where the solar wind convective electric field points away from Mars. This pronounced spatial asymmetry indicates that the convective electric field plays a major role in beam formation. In the solar wind and magnetosheath, these beams are consistent with the plume. Their occurrence-weighted flux agrees with previous long-term plume estimates, whereas the flux during identified beam intervals is substantially higher. This highlights the advantage of our event-based approach in capturing the intrinsic properties of O2+ plumes. In the induced magnetotail, beam formation remains strongly associated with the convective electric field, while the Hall electric field can provide additional energization. The beam properties are primarily controlled by upstream solar wind conditions, with no apparent influence from solar extreme ultraviolet irradiance. These results might improve our understanding of ion escape at Mars. More broadly, we also demonstrate the potential of deep learning for identifying transient plasma populations from spacecraft observations.
