CoPiT: A conditional Position-induced Transformer for aerodynamic field prediction on unstructured meshes

Junfeng Chen, Yongchen Fan, Zhonghua Qiao, Kailiang Wu

arXiv:2610.32299·physics.comp-ph·Published 2026-10-23

High-fidelity Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes (RANS) simulations remain a standard tool for aerodynamic analysis, yet each solve on an unstructured mesh is computationally expensive, making many-query tasks such as design exploration, optimization, and multi-condition evaluation costly. Neural operators provide mesh-flexible surrogates for mapping geometry and operating conditions to flow fields; however, in many existing architectures, global parameters such as the freestream state and design variables are concatenated with pointwise inputs, leaving their influence on intermediate features implicit. This paper proposes CoPiT, a \textbf{Co}nditional \textbf{P}osition-induced \textbf{T}ransformer that builds on the Position-induced Transformer (PiT) as its geometric backbone and injects a global condition vector through multi-stage feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM), thereby making parametric modulation explicit while preserving geometry-based attention weights. The model is evaluated on two-dimensional AirfRANS airfoil fields, including scarce-data and extrapolation regimes, and on three-dimensional BlendedNet++ blended-wing-body surfaces. On the AirfRANS full-data test set, CoPiT reduces surface and volume losses by approximately 54\% and 24\% relative to Transolver, respectively, and achieves the lowest overall normalized test MSE among the compared baselines on BlendedNet++. Matched ablations show that FiLM conditioning generally improves upon input concatenation, while the preferred placement is task-dependent. CoPiT thus provides a practical operator-learning surrogate for parametric aerodynamics on unstructured meshes.
