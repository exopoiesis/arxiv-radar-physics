A Differentiable Framework for Inverse Identification of Heterogeneous Cohesive Laws

Sida Hao, Jinkyo Han, Bahador Bahmani

arXiv:2610.33711·physics.comp-ph·Published 2026-10-24

This work develops a differentiable finite element framework based on an intrinsic cohesive-zone formulation for identifying traction-separation laws from global force-displacement histories and spatially sparse strain measurements. The framework employs physics-constrained neural networks to represent flexible, thermodynamically structured cohesive laws, with sensitivities computed through automatic differentiation and a discrete adjoint that accounts for the history-dependent evolution of interfacial damage. To enable identification of spatially heterogeneous cohesive behavior, we extend learned constitutive priors to irreversible interfacial damage by constructing a shared, low-dimensional family of cohesive laws from representative traction-separation histories. This representation exploits common constitutive structure and parameter sharing to reduce the number of unknowns and regularize the inverse problem, while allowing local cohesive behavior to vary spatially through latent coordinates. Numerical investigations involving double-cantilever beams and fiber-matrix composites demonstrate that accurate reproduction of global force-displacement responses does not necessarily imply accurate recovery of the underlying cohesive laws. Incorporating spatially resolved strain measurements substantially improves constitutive identification, even when the admissible responses are constrained by a learned prior. Comparisons with conventional bilinear cohesive models and Bayesian optimization further illustrate the benefits of the learned constitutive representation and adjoint-based identification for high-dimensional inverse problems.
