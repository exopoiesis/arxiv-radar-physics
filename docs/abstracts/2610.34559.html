A Product-Grassmann Tucker Surrogate for Parametric Field Prediction

Melika Baghi, Xiao Liu, Kamran Paynabar

arXiv:2610.34559·cs.CE·Published 2026-10-25

Many-query applications such as design optimization, uncertainty quantification and digital twins call for repeated full-field predictions from expensive partial differential equation (PDE) simulations. Grassmannian reduced-order models interpolate parameter-dependent subspaces but commonly vectorize the spatial field, and the ambient dimension then grows with the product of the grid sizes. Tensor-based models preserve the multiway structure but often use shared mode-wise bases or a fixed core across parameters. We propose a Tucker-based surrogate that keeps the spatial directions and time as separate modes. The factor subspaces are interpolated on a product Grassmann manifold, Procrustes alignment places the Tucker cores in one coordinate system, and a truncated singular value decomposition models their variation. Across five PDE benchmarks in two and three spatial dimensions, including public computational fluid dynamics data, the interpolation size is reduced by factors of 13 to 482 and the stored model by factors of 13 to 464 relative to a single-subspace Grassmannian surrogate with the same local interpolation. Cached queries run 3.3 to 8.5 times faster, the mean errors over ten resampled splits differ by at most 0.22 percentage points (0.36 when every model choice is re-selected inside each split), and the prediction-error terms of the two methods differ by less than 0.1 percentage points. The remaining accuracy differences are consistent with the larger representation error of the Tucker format. In a three-dimensional grid study the interpolation-size ratio rises to 2,260 and the query speedup to 11.9 at 64^3.
