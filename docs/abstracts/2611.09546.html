Through Ups and Downs: the Transient Lensing Behaviour of SIDM halos undergoing Core Collapse

Frank C. van den Bosch, Yarone M. Tokayer

arXiv:2611.09546·astro-ph.CO·Published 2026-11-06

Self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) haloes can become extremely dense following gravothermal core collapse, motivating their use as explanations for unusually concentrated gravitational lenses. Using an emulator calibrated on gravothermal fluid calculations, we investigate whether increasing central density necessarily strengthens lensing. We follow initially Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) haloes through core formation and collapse deep into the short-mean-free-path regime, and compute finite-source magnifications, critical curves, and source-plane lensing cross sections for isolated spherical haloes with elastic, velocity-independent self-interactions, including external shear in selected calculations. Lensing strength evolves non-monotonically: it initially decreases during core formation, rises during early collapse, and generally declines at later stages despite continued growth of the central density. This decline accompanies the emergence of a dense inner core whose mass and radius decrease within a more extended, lower-density core. Time-averaged enhancement relative to NFW haloes depends on the lensing statistic, halo concentration, and interaction cross section. Generalized NFW haloes with central cusps $ρ\propto r^{-γ}$ with $γ\geq 2$ always have larger lensing efficacies than core-collapsed SIDM haloes of equal mass and are therefore poor proxies, including in terms of the demographics (size and radial thickness) of arcs and Einstein rings. The core collapse stage at which SIDM haloes are optimal lenses coincides with the epoch at which the characteristic core surface density is close to critical, which typically occurs during the early stages of core collapse. These results demonstrate that central density alone is insufficient to characterize SIDM lensing and that its evolving mass distribution must be modelled explicitly.
