State and spatial completion of 1D premixed flames for hydrogen and methane combustion

Federico Rios Tascon, Ryan F. Johnson, Diego D. Ortiz, Peter J. Schmid, Beverley J. McKeon

arXiv:2611.10029·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-05

Accurate prediction of chemically reacting flows requires resolving the nonlinear coupling among transport and chemical reaction operators, often at substantial computational cost. This work investigates Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) for learning mappings from partial spatial profiles to steady, one-dimensional, freely propagating hydrogen-air and methane-air flame solutions. Using a database of over 15,000 cases generated with Cantera, two different FNO models were trained to reconstruct the complete thermochemical state from a smaller set of input variables. The first model concerns state completion which uses only temperature, fuel, and oxygen mass-fraction profiles to reconstruct the remaining 7 species for hydrogen flames and 51 species for methane flames along with the missing velocity. The second model performs spatial completion, using upstream thermochemical profiles extending into the initial temperature rise to reconstruct the withheld downstream structure. Evaluation on 2,625 test cases per fuel, excluded from training, shows recovery of reaction-zone species profiles, including radicals and intermediates, across the sampled conditions. These results demonstrate reconstruction of species and velocity profiles that are not supplied as inputs, and recovery of downstream structure outside the observed region. Reconstruction with missing species or incomplete spatial coverage suggests potential for using learned operators in estimating inaccessible thermochemical quantities and developing reduced-state representations for combustion applications.
