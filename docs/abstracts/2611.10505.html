Reconstructing Interacting Blood Flow and Wall Interface Deformation from Noisy Flow Data Using PirateNets

Han Zhang, Xue-Cheng Tai, Alessandro Veneziani, Raymond H. Chan

arXiv:2611.10505·physics.flu-dyn·Published 2026-11-07

Blood flow and elastic vessel-wall motion interact with each other: fluid stress drives wall deformation, while the moving wall changes the domain in which blood flows. Given noisy flow images, we study the inverse problem of reconstructing the coupled, unsteady three-dimensional motions. We solve the reconstruction through Gauss--Seidel-type alternating fluid and geometry updates. For the fluid, we use physics-informed networks to recover velocity and pressure by fitting the observations while enforcing the incompressible Navier--Stokes equations on the moving domain, which is described by an arbitrary Lagrangian--Eulerian (ALE) map. For the geometry, a reference-domain correction and sequential registration recover the vessel shape and wall displacement. Interface traction continuity and an elastic momentum equation regularize the registration so that the recovered displacement is also physics-informed. The networks evaluate fields and physical residuals at sampled space--time points without remeshing the moving domain. Experiments on a converging channel and a patient-like aorta demonstrate reconstruction of flow and wall motion from noisy data. Ablation and parameter-sensitivity studies examine the role of structure regularization and imperfect physical coefficients. A further experiment with a three-element Windkessel outlet condition demonstrates how the framework accommodates a more physiologically realistic boundary setting.
