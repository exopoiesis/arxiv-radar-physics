On-the-Fly Reduced Bases Through the Lens of Augmented Krylov Methods

Philippe-André Luneau

arXiv:2611.11129·math.NA·Published 2026-11-08

On-the-fly reduced order models (ROMs) are widely used in applications where multiple queries of large-scale models are required in an iterative method, like PDE-constrained optimization or fluid-structure interaction. When compared to classical offline--online ROMs, they have the advantage of being agnostic to any precomputed training data, mesh or geometry, which makes them more general linear/nonlinear solver accelerators. The main goal of this work is to study the effect of using a reduced basis constructed on-the-fly to accelerate a restarted Krylov algorithm, and to formally bridge this category of data-driven methods with classical augmented Krylov methods. The guiding principles of on-the-fly reduced basis will be translated into the framework of Krylov subspace methods, for a Petrov-Galerkin projection method. Previous corrections generated by the algorithm are stored in a snapshot matrix, and a singular value decomposition (SVD) is used to determine a reduced subspace containing the most significant directions. It will be shown that this approach can be embedded in the framework of Krylov methods with augmented or deflated restart, but with a different, data-driven recycling strategy. An analytical study will be presented to determine the consequences of augmentation by ROM-based vectors. Numerical experiments on a convection-diffusion problem with various degrees of convection will show that the proposed methodology yields results comparable to classical iterative linear solvers in some regimes, especially for nearly symmetric or skew-symmetric operators. At equal sizes of the augmentation basis, the convergence was sometimes faster with the proposed framework than with deflation methods.
