Machine learning assisted $ν/\barν$ discrimination in atmospheric neutrino events for CPV sensitivity in a 20-kton liquid scintillator detector

Dek Hao Liew, Mohammad Nizam, Shu Tian Eu, Siew Yan Hoh

arXiv:2611.11474·hep-ex·Published 2026-11-08

Atmospheric neutrinos span a broad range of energies and baselines, offering a complementary probe of leptonic CP violation. However, a JUNO-like liquid scintillator detector without a magnetic field cannot directly separate neutrino and antineutrino interactions, reducing CP violation sensitivity. This study investigates whether machine learning based statistical separation can improve CP violation sensitivity in a 20-kton detector. $ν_μ$ and $ν_e$ charged current events are generated with GENIE (using Honda flux) in a JUNO-like detector geometry and are reweighted using OscProb three flavor oscillation probabilities incorporating Earth matter effects and NuFIT parameters. Detector response is approximated by smearing lepton and hadronic energies, neutrino direction, and neutron multiplicity. Logistic regression, XGBoost, and multilayer perceptron (MLP) classifiers are trained on inelasticity, neutron multiplicity, and charged lepton energy, with XGBoost selected for the analysis. CP violation sensitivity is evaluated for a 200 kton-year exposure using a Poisson likelihood with systematic nuisance parameters and marginalization over five oscillation parameters within their respective $3σ$ ranges. XGBoost achieves an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of approximately 0.83 for both flavor samples. Under the square root approximation, peak CP violation sensitivity improves from 1.49 $σ$ to 1.96 $σ$ for normal mass ordering and from 1.14 $σ$ to 1.61 $σ$ for inverted mass ordering. Machine learning based statistical neutrino-antineutrino separation can recover part of the CP violation sensitivity lost in charge blind detectors. These preliminary results motivate further studies incorporating validated detector response and systematic uncertainties.
