Nonlinear integral equations for stress-controlled rheology: Collocation methods for Volterra equations with functional kernels

Stephan Domann, Thomas Voigtmann

arXiv:2611.12578·cond-mat.soft·Published 2026-11-09

We present a class of numerical collocation methods to solve a typical inverse problem that arises in the stress-controlled rheology of complex fluids with pronounced history-dependent response, where the relation between stress and flow rate is given by an integral constitutive equation as the material law. We demonstrate the method by calculating the creep response of a nontrivial model of a shear-thinning viscoelastic fluid where a stress overshoot in rate-controlled startup flow leads to ``sudden yielding'' in stress-controlled creep. Our method is able to address the long-time behavior of the model, distinguishing the amorphous solid from the stress-induced yielded fluid, and from the in-between case of infinite deformation that never achieves a stationary shear rate (proper creep). To this end, we implement a method with flexible time-stepping, and prove uniqueness of the solution and convergence of the methods for a broad class of highly viscoelastic non-linear rheological laws in integral form.
