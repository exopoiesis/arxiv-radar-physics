AlphaHits: Full-Event Tracking and Reconstruction Towards a Hit-Level Foundation Model

Sascha Caron, Nadezhda Dobreva, Polina Moskvitina, Eugene Shalugin, Javad Sohrabi, Roberto Ruiz, Yue Zhao

arXiv:2611.13222·hep-ex·Published 2026-11-09

We introduce AlphaHits, a Transformer-based approach towards hit-level foundation models for LHC physics, building on the TrackFormer and HiggsFormer architectures. AlphaHits operates directly on detector hits and demonstrates scalable full-event charged-particle reconstruction and multi-task event reconstruction and classification from low-level detector information. AlphaHits-TrackFormer reconstructs the full charged-particle content of complete TrackML events without imposing a transverse-momentum selection on the target tracks. It processes both pixel and strip hits in events containing up to $1.5\times10^5$ hits under HL-LHC-like pileup conditions and assigns hits to individual reconstructed trajectories across its kinematic range. This includes the challenging low-$p_T$ population down to approximately $150\,\mathrm{MeV}$ in TrackML samples. It achieves a TrackML score of 0.959 over all reconstructable tracks, representing, to our knowledge, the highest reported TrackML score for full-detector, inclusive charged-particle reconstruction. Building on the TrackFormer architecture, AlphaHits is pretrained on charged-particle tracking and fine-tuned for multi-task reconstruction and classification. From a shared hit-level representation, it predicts tracks, jets, jet flavour, and event-level observables. AlphaHits achieves an AUC of 0.944 for Higgs event classification, with performance comparable to a Particle Transformer (ParT) baseline using fully reconstructed inputs. It also achieves performance comparable to Delphes-based baselines for jet reconstruction, jet flavour classification, and missing transverse momentum regression. These results highlight the potential for scalable full-event tracking and multi-task learning directly from detector hits, representing a promising step towards transferable hit-level representations and general-purpose foundation models for LHC physics.
