Self-Interacting Neutrinos in NEDE and DRMD Cosmologies: Posteriors, profiles and evidence using CLiENT

Magnus Uggerhøj, Tobias Johannessen, Jeppe Pagaard Thybo, Steen Hannestad, Thomas Tram

arXiv:2611.13756·astro-ph.CO·Published 2026-11-10

A large number of extensions of the $Λ$CDM model have been proposed as solutions to the Hubble tension, but they are almost always tested separately. Combinations of such models are rarely studied, and one reason is that their parameter spaces are very costly to explore with standard methods. Here we study a combination of two models which have each been proposed as a solution: self-interacting neutrinos (SI$ν$) and the dark radiation matter decoupling (DRMD) of hot new early dark energy. The SI$ν$+DRMD model has twelve cosmological parameters, a bimodal posterior in the neutrino self-interaction and several flat directions in parameter space, and it can only be investigated in detail because we use the likelihood emulator CLiENT. With emulators trained on about $3\times 10^5$ likelihood evaluations per dataset we obtain posteriors, best fits and profile likelihoods for the model and for the models nested within it, and all quoted $χ^2$ values are calculated with the true likelihood. We use two data combinations, both with and without the SH0ES calibration: a conservative one consisting of Planck, DESI BAO and Pantheon+ supernovae, and a more aggressive one in which the small-scale CMB spectra come from ACT and SPT. With the most aggressive combination, the SI$ν$+DRMD model provides a much better fit than either SI$ν$ or DRMD on its own, and its posterior gives $H_0 = 70.94\pm 0.60$ km/s/Mpc. It lowers the $χ^2$ of $Λ$CDM by 27.6, compared with 9.9 for DRMD and 4.0 for SI$ν$, i.e. by more than the sum of the two ($Δ$AIC = -15.7 relative to DRMD, in terms of the Akaike information criterion). This shows that combined models can fit the data considerably better than the individual models, and it demonstrates the value of being able to analyse such complex models in detail.
