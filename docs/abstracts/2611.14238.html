Unveiling the Extreme Universe with Recent IACT Discoveries

R. López-Coto

arXiv:2611.14238·astro-ph.HE·Published 2026-11-10

Very-high-energy (VHE, $E \gtrsim 50$\,GeV) $γ$-ray astronomy has evolved into an indispensable pillar of modern astroparticle physics, offering a direct view of non-thermal particle acceleration, cosmic-ray origins, and fundamental physics. Ground-based Imaging Atmospheric Cherenkov Telescopes (IACTs) have driven this revolution over the past two decades. In this plenary review, presented at the 9th Heidelberg International Symposium on High-Energy Gamma-Ray Astronomy (Gamma 2026), I provide a synthesis of recent breakthrough physics discoveries achieved by current IACT arrays (H.E.S.S., MAGIC, and VERITAS) alongside early scientific achievements from the Large-Sized Telescope prototype (LST-1) of the Cherenkov Telescope Array Observatory (CTAO). Key highlights include the definitive establishment of recurrent novae as transient cosmic-ray proton accelerators via RS Ophiuchi; spatially resolved multi-TeV emission from the relativistic jet termination shocks of microquasars; the discovery of a 20 TeV pulsed radiation component from the Vela pulsar, challenging established magnetospheric emission paradigms; precision measurements of the Galactic Center diffuse ridge spectrum favoring a high-energy cutoff; the detection of distant flat-spectrum radio quasars at $z \sim 1$ (OP 313 and PKS 0346-27), providing tight cosmological constraints on the Extragalactic Background Light; multi-wavelength afterglow detections of $γ$-ray bursts including GRB 221009A; and world-leading joint indirect dark matter limits from dwarf spheroidal galaxy and Galactic Center halo analyses. Together with algorithmic advances in deep learning reconstruction and the ongoing construction of CTAO, these milestones demonstrate the enduring vitality and future trajectory of ground-based $γ$-ray astrophysics.
