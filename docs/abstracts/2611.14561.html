Provenance-tracked, experimentally validated neural operator surrogates of three-dimensional laser melting with keyholes and free-form paths

Alix Benoit, Elia Iseli

arXiv:2611.14561·cs.CE·Published 2026-11-05

A three-dimensional thermo-fluid-dynamic simulation of laser melting resolves the melt pool and vapor depression but costs from one to thousands of core-hours per track. We train FNO, RIGNO and GAOT neural operators that predict the temperature, melt-pool and free-surface fields. Our datasets comprise 3D temperature and melt geometry fields covering lack-of-fusion to keyhole conditions under varied laser power, scan speed, beam radius, beam shape and preheat, and include free-form scan paths with speed changes and up to four beams. We generate them with FLOW-3D WELD, using solver settings validated against published measurements for four materials. We implement an AiiDA framework to store the provenance of every simulation, model and evaluation, so each tabulated metric traces back to the solver and model inputs behind it. On held-out simulations, the best architecture scores below 2 % relative temperature error on quasi-steady straight tracks, below 4 % on single-beam free-form paths and about 5 % with two to four beams. Inference takes under a second per predicted arbitrary time step on our single-GPU setup. We extend a surrogate trained on Ti-6Al-4V to Inconel 718 by growing its training set autonomously, and the extended models reach a relative temperature error of about 3 % or less on both Inconel 718 and Ti-6Al-4V. At 20 Ti-6Al-4V and Inconel 718 conditions held out of training, the two-alloy RIGNO predicts melt-pool and vapor-depression depths within 30 % of published measurements, and its depths differ from FLOW-3D at the same conditions by 2 % on average. Furthermore, these results provide a scalable framework which can readily be applied to computationally intensive simulations in fields other than laser melting.
