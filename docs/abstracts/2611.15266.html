DaYu-TC: a global-regional nested AI system for tropical cyclone forecasting

Runzepu Hou, Feng Zhang, Xin Wu, Xujun Wei, Fenghua Ling, Kan Dai, Gaozhen Nie, Leiming Ma, Renhe Zhang

arXiv:2611.15266·physics.ao-ph·Published 2026-11-11

Accurate tropical cyclone intensity forecasts are essential for disaster preparedness, yet persistent intensity underestimation limits the value of artificial intelligence (AI) weather models for predicting these destructive storms. Here we present DaYu-TC, a global-regional nested system that links the preservation of strong-wind and low-pressure signals with high-resolution regional prediction. Its global and regional models share an architecture and extreme-aware training objectives. The regional model combines evolving local states with global forecasts valid at each target time, connecting large-scale environmental information with finer-scale cyclone evolution. In an independent evaluation of 16 western North Pacific tropical cyclones in 2023, the global model retains competitive conventional forecast skill while reducing five-day maximum-wind root-mean-square error (RMSE) by 6-22% relative to other evaluated global AI models. Relative to the global model, regional nesting reduces maximum-wind RMSE by 22.0-33.1% and minimum-pressure RMSE by 28.6-39.8% across 12-120 h. The complete system outperforms concurrent operational high-resolution forecasts from the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts for minimum pressure at 12-120 h and maximum wind speed at 48-96 h, with respective RMSE reductions of 26% and 12% at 72 h. Without retraining, the same regional model enhances three other global AI models, reducing overall maximum-wind RMSE by 22.6-28.6%. Five-day forecasts take under one minute on a single GPU. These results establish a transferable multiscale approach to reducing cyclone intensity underestimation, extending advances in AI weather forecasting towards more accurate prediction of high-impact weather events.
