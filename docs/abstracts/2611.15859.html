Learning Quantum Hamiltonians from the Dynamical Structure Factor

Nisarga Paul, Thomas Schuster, Jason Alicea

arXiv:2611.15859·cond-mat.str-el·Published 2026-11-11

Identifying the microscopic interactions governing a quantum system is essential for developing predictive theories of materials and validating quantum technologies. Despite considerable progress, learning Hamiltonians from realistic experimental data remains a challenge. Motivated by neutron scattering and quantum simulations, we study the problem of learning a quantum Hamiltonian from the dynamical structure factor (DSF), which captures two-point correlation data in quantum magnets. We show that the DSF encodes sufficient information to efficiently reconstruct broad classes of quantum Hamiltonians. Our approach exploits simple relations between low-order spectral moments of the DSF and microscopic couplings, giving rise to three complementary Hamiltonian tomography protocols based on the high-temperature expansion, strong-field polarization, and the fluctuation-dissipation theorem, respectively. The framework we introduce establishes practical and robust Hamiltonian learning from common experimental data, with potentially immediate application to neutron scattering experiments.
