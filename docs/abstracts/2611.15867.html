Ensemble Learning with Unsupervised and Semi-Supervised Models for New Physics Searches at Colliders

Alexandre Alves, Eduardo da Silva Almeida, Douglas Roberto de Matos Pimentel

arXiv:2611.15867·hep-ph·Published 2026-11-11

New physics beyond the Standard Model is widely believed to exist motivated by key limitations of the current theory. However, in the absence of undisputed experimental signals, there is little guidance on what type of extended physics model should be built. To address the challenge of training machine learning classifiers to spot unknown phenomena, many algorithms have been applied to the task of either identifying anomalous events relative to background expectations or cluster collision events together in classes of similarity. However, as in the supervised case, the no free lunch theorem assures that the performance of unsupervised models depends on the specific signal hypothesis and their inductive biases, and the successful identification of a given signal by a particular algorithm does not guarantee comparable performance for other signals. In this study, we take a step toward a more model-independent unsupervised and semi-supervised search strategy by combining classifiers into ensembles. Our goal is addressing two complementary aspects of unsupervised ensemble learning - overall performance and robustness of the classification task from a thorough assessment of ensemble models. We show that, despite sacrificing some performance relative to the best individual model in the majority of cases, the ensembles provide robust and reliable classification predictions across several signal hypotheses, increasing confidence that genuinely unknown new physics will not pass unnoticed.
