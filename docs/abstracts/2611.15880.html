Identification of extragalactic radio sources in the VISTA survey of the Magellanic Clouds using an unsupervised machine learning algorithm

Clara M. Pennock, Jacco Th. van Loon, Mohammed Iddrisu Nlowie, Elizabeth Joy Leese, Maja Nowaczyk, Heinz Andernach, Duncan Farrah, Valentin D. Ivanov, Michał J. Michałowski, Joana M. Oliveira, Jahang Prathap, Syed F. Rahman, Mara Salvato, Tayyaba Zafar

arXiv:2611.15880·astro-ph.GA·Published 2026-11-11

The Magellanic Clouds benefit from a wealth of data across the electromagnetic spectrum, but extracting extragalactic sources from these crowded fields is a less than straightforward endeavour. We used an unsupervised machine learning algorithm (t-distributed stochastic neighbourhood embedding; t-SNE) to classify 6,652 and 1,356 sources in the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds, respectively, that were detected in the VISTA Survey of the Magellanic Clouds (VMC) and the Evolutionary Map of the Universe (EMU) survey with the Australian Square Kilometre Array Pathfinder (ASKAP). Multi-wavelength photometry and colours from optical to radio were used as features to cluster similar sources together in 2D space on a t-SNE map. We used previously classified sources to separate the clusters into five classes: AGN, low-redshift galaxies, blazars, dusty stellar objects (e.g. young stellar objects, planetary nebulæ etc) and high proper-motion stars. Previous photometric classifications from the literature match with specific clusters identified, as well as provide insight on areas of the t-SNE map for which there are no previous classifications. This work shows that the combination of unsupervised machine learning with photometry from optical to radio has proven adept at separating the stellar from the extragalactic, and the AGN from the galaxies. Our associated new spectroscopy with SALT includes new discoveries of AGN up to redshift 3.3 and Magellanic emission-line objects.
