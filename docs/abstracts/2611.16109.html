Understanding the intrinsic 3D shapes of galaxies provides crucial insights into their formation and evolution, but determining these from projections remains challenging. We apply a Bayesian inference framework to optical images of galaxies from the Euclid Data Release 1, leveraging its large sample size to test the scalability and robustness of this methodology. Applying Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) sampling to Sérsic axis ratios and effective radii, we constrain the population-level mean 3D ellipticity, triaxiality, and size, together with their covariances for $40\,864\,304$ star-forming galaxies with stellar masses $M_\ast=10^{9}$--$10^{10.5}M_\odot$ at redshifts $0<z<1.2$. Our sample size exceeds previous shape inference studies by factors of $440$--$52\,800$ relative to early observations by JWST, and factors of $440$--$17\,600$ relative to legacy observations from HST in overlapping mass-redshift bins, enabling finer binning alongside improved statistical constraints on shape evolution. We additionally include over 9 million galaxies at $z<0.5$, a regime historically limited by sample size in previous shape inference studies. We recover key evolutionary trends identified in previous smaller-sample studies. We find high ellipticities ($μ_E \sim 0.600$--$0.821$) across all bins, indicating that the underlying 3D shape distributions are statistically dominated by flattened geometries. Most notably, we recover the decline of the prolate fraction towards $z=0$ at low masses ($M_\ast=10^{9}$--$10^{9.5}M_\odot$) reported by previous studies. This trend is seen consistently in comparable analyses of independent data sets spanning different depths and resolution regimes, showing that it is not an artefact of small-sample statistics, suggesting it may be a genuine feature of galaxy evolution. We also identify future methodological improvements for subsequent Euclid data releases.
