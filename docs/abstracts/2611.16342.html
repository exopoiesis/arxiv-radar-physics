HARMONY: an efficient and accurate deep learning framework for prediction of experimental vibrational spectra

Xinyu Lu, Bowen Li, Yi Rong, Qingling Zhou, Xingbo Tang, Menghan Li, Yuqiang Li, Guokun Liu, Tong Zhu, Bin Ren, Hao Ma

arXiv:2611.16342·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-12

Predicting experimental vibrational spectra requires both efficient molecular calculations and a reliable mapping from theoretical vibrations to measured signals. Here we introduce \textbf{HARMONY}, a two-stage deep-learning framework that bridges this gap using harmonic spectra as physical anchors. Equivariant neural networks directly predict atomic forces and spectroscopic tensors for rapid harmonic-spectrum construction, while HarmoNet combines these spectra with molecular topology and measurement metadata to predict experimental infrared and Raman spectra. HARMONY achieves mean cosine similarities of 0.912 for infrared and 0.907 for Raman spectra, with a mean inference time of 2.12~s per molecule, approximately 600-fold faster than the evaluated DFT workflow. Beyond forward prediction, HARMONY improves inverse molecular inference through pseudo-experimental pretraining and spectral verification of candidate structures. Integrating HARMONY and Vib2Mol as scientific tools within a general-purpose agent enables adaptive candidate generation and verification. With molecular formulas provided, the resulting Vib2Mol--HARMONY Agent achieves Top-1 recall of 70.53\% on NIST using infrared spectra and 83.33\% on SDBS using combined infrared and Raman spectra. Fine-tuning with 19 amino-acid controls and eight peptides further increases mean Raman cosine similarity from 0.669 to 0.896 on 15 held-out peptides. HARMONY establishes harmonic anchoring as a practical connection between molecular physics, experimental spectroscopy and adaptive molecular structure elucidation.
