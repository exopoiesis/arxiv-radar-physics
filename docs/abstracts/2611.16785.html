Bayesian Inference of Fermionic Dark Matter Admixed Neutron Stars Informed by Galaxy Cluster Constraints

H. C. Das, J. B. Wei

arXiv:2611.16785·astro-ph.HE·Published 2026-11-12

Self-interacting dark matter is constrained on galactic and cluster scales by its elastic scattering cross section per unit mass, $σ/m_χ\sim 0.1-10\ {\rm cm^2/g}$. We incorporate this cross section into the equation of state of an asymmetric, self-interacting fermionic dark matter component and perform a Bayesian inference of dark matter admixed neutron stars in the two-fluid framework at $σ/m_χ= 0.1$, $1$ and $10\ {\rm cm^2/g}$. Seven nuclear matter parameters are sampled together with the dark particle mass and mass fraction, and confronted with nuclear theory and neutron star observations. The nuclear sector and the global observables are statistically indistinguishable across the whole range of cross sections, while the data constrain the dark matter mass to $m_χ\simeq 1.45-1.88$~GeV and its mass fraction to $f_χ\simeq 0.05-0.09$, depending on the cross section. Finally, comparing runs built from the same parameter draws, so that only the cross section differs, we find that it does change the baryonic radius appreciably, but only for light dark matter, a region the astrophysical data largely disfavor. The self-interaction is therefore not too weak to be seen; however its effect is hidden in the region of parameter space that the present observational data already disfavor.
