MID: a muon detector for the ALICE 3 upgrade project

Jesus Eduardo Muñoz Mendez

arXiv:2611.17363·physics.ins-det·Published 2026-11-05

The success of the ALICE programme has allowed to study the hottest state of matter created in the laboratory: the strongly-interacting quark-gluon plasma (sQGP). However, some fundamental questions on the sQGP and other aspects of the strong interaction will remain open even after LHC Run 4. To address this, the ALICE collaboration has proposed a major upgrade of its detector, known as ALICE 3. Among the subdetectors considered for this upgrade is the muon identification detector (MID), whose aim is to contribute to the study of charmonium and exotic-hadron production in heavy-ion collisions. While the baseline design consists of plastic-scintillator-based chambers placed around a cylindrical magnetic iron absorber, different detector technologies are under evaluation for the MID, including multi-wire proportional chambers. This proceeding presents the results of two MID prototypes based on FNAL-NICADD scintillator bars, Kuraray fibers and Hamamatsu SiPMs, characterized in test beams at the CERN Proton Synchrotron with 3 GeV/$c$ muon- and pion-enriched beams. The muon efficiency as well as the hadron suppression obtained using machine learning techniques will be discussed.
