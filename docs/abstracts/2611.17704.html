An AI-enabled high-resolution chlorophyll-a forecasting model that outperforms the operational global biogeochemical system

Yong Jiang, Zheng Jiang, Gaowei Zhang, Zengzhou Hao, Suixiang Shi, Chi Zhang, Wei Wang

arXiv:2611.17704·cs.AI·Published 2026-11-13·Updated 2026-11-16

Surface chlorophyll-a concentration is a key indicator of marine phytoplankton variability, and short-term forecasts of its distribution provide advance information for marine ecosystem monitoring and management. However, interactions between physical transport and biological processes complicate chlorophyll-a forecasting, and simulating these coupled processes with conventional numerical models imposes substantial computational demands. Here we introduce ChlCast, an AI model that generates daily forecasts of surface chlorophyll-a concentration over a 10-day horizon at approximately 4-km resolution between 60$^\circ$S and 60$^\circ$N. ChlCast combines factorized spatiotemporal convolutions with a multiscale architecture to learn from historical chlorophyll-a fields and climatological information. Compared with the operational 0.25$^\circ$ Copernicus global ocean biogeochemical forecasting system, ChlCast reduces mean absolute error and root mean square error by 70.74% and 47.63%, respectively, against satellite-derived Level-4 chlorophyll-a fields, and by 35.03% and 13.07% against independent BGC-Argo in situ observations. Coastal case studies further show that the 4-km forecasts retain coastal gradients and localized concentration maxima that are weakened by coarse-grid averaging. These results demonstrate the potential of observation-driven deep learning for high-resolution forecasting of surface chlorophyll-a variability at the near-global scale.
