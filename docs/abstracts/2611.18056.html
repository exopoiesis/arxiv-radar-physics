Unsupervised Machine Learning Classification of FRB 20121102A across Distinct Active Episodes: Implications for FRB Emission Physics and Source Environments

Xiao Li, En-Wei Liang

arXiv:2611.18056·astro-ph.HE·Published 2026-11-13

Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are millisecond-duration radio transients of extragalactic origin. Identifying distinct burst populations and investigating their variations across active episodes provide important insights into the physical origins of repeating FRBs. In this paper, we apply an unsupervised machine learning approach to bursts from three active episodes of FRB 20121102A observed by the Five-hundred-metre Aperture Spherical Telescope (FAST). Our analysis reveals four distinct clusters primarily distinguished by their spectral properties. Notably, Cluster 1 exhibits bimodal-like distributions in central frequency and bandwidth and contains nearly all high-energy bursts, with a mean energy about an order of magnitude higher than those of the other clusters. It also occupies a distinct region in the width--fluence parameter space. These results suggest that this cluster may represent a distinct burst population potentially associated with different emission physics. Furthermore, the fractions of different burst clusters vary across active episodes. Cluster 1 accounts for about 27\% of the bursts in 2019, compared with about 8\% in 2022 and 2023. Cluster 2, with higher central frequencies and narrower bandwidths, has the largest fraction in 2019 and 2022, whereas Cluster 3, with broader bandwidths and intermediate central frequencies, has the largest fraction in 2023. These variations may indicate changes in the physical conditions of the emission regions or the local propagation environment across active episodes.
