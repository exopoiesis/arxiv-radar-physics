Generalized hydraulic neural operators turbocharging sewer digital twins

Ruozhou Lin, Shixun Li, Tao Shi, Lihan Hu, Zhiyu Zhang, Wenchong Tian, Zhiguo Yuan

arXiv:2611.18081·cs.CE·Published 2026-11-13

Hydraulic digital twins are emerging as the next-generation paradigm for operating and managing sewer systems. Conventional hydraulic digital twinning relies on running physics-based models (e.g., Saint-Venant equations), which are computationally expensive and often too slow for real-time applications. Although surrogates (e.g., neural networks) can be fast, existing approaches are typically non-generalizable, requiring costly retraining for unseen pipes, networks, or flow conditions. Here, we develop a fast, generalized and differentiable multi-branch neural operator trained on solutions of the Saint-Venant equations that predicts in-pipe hydraulics. The learned operator reproduces complex hydraulic dynamics with high fidelity while achieving thousand- to million-fold speedups for online prediction, data assimilation, and diagnostics. Equally importantly, the learned operator generalizes to unseen pipes, networks, and flow conditions without retraining. These advances address key computational barriers to real-time sewer digital twins, opening new opportunities for more responsive flood-risk management and protection of urban water environments.
