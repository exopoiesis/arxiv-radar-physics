Structure makes quantum circuits trainable

Wenbo Xie, Wojciech Szpankowski, Ananth Grama

arXiv:2611.18378·quant-ph·Published 2026-11-13

The scalability of variational quantum algorithms is limited by barren plateaus, where gradients vanish exponentially in system size. This worst-case result raises fundamental questions about the trainability of quantum AI models. We present a major advance towards resolving this trainability question by answering whether a given quantum circuit has a barren plateau. We do this using an analytical framework that bounds the gradient variance for arbitrary input states $ρ$ and observables $\mathcal{M}$ by decomposing the unitary representation of the associated compact Lie group. When the circuit segments on both sides of a gate form $2$-designs, we obtain the variance exactly and prove a sufficient condition for trainability: if $ρ^{\otimes 2}$ and $\mathcal{M}^{\otimes 2}$ are sufficiently concentrated on the same irreducible invariant subspace of polynomial dimension, and the differentiated gate moves this subspace by an inverse-polynomial amount, then the gradient variance is at least inverse-polynomial. We further present a sampling algorithm that estimates the gradient variance from runs of the ansatz, without constructing the representation-theoretic projectors. Our algorithm decides whether the variance exceeds a tolerance $τ$ with the number of runs polynomial in $1/τ$, including cases in which the bounds are inconclusive. This resolves the question of the trainability of the circuit before expensive training procedures.
