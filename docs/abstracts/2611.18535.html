A Surrogate Boundary Condition for Improved Accuracy and Performance of Local Modeling in Radiation Transport Simulations

Jonathan Trieloff, Ethan Peterson

arXiv:2611.18535·physics.comp-ph·Published 2026-11-13

Monte Carlo neutronics analysis of tokamak designs is computationally expensive due to the complex CAD geometries required to accurately model them. Design iteration of port plugs using global transport in the full model is impractical because of the computational cost. To circumvent this, local modeling methods are used. In the local modeling method, a boundary condition is assumed on the port plug, such that particles that leave the port plug do not require transport in the surrounding global model. For this purpose, vacuum and reflective boundaries are assumed, both of which provide poor approximation of the true transport physics in the global model. To improve on current techniques, a surrogate model is presented that provides a physics-informed boundary condition for the component. A neural network is trained to reproduce the boundary condition on the component that represents the behavior of particles returning from the surrounding geometry. Monte Carlo transport is only necessary within the component, and approximate physics from the surrounding region are retained. This approach allows the design in the component to be iterated without requiring full model transport and joining of the component mesh to the global mesh in each iteration. In this work, the surrogate model is evaluated against vacuum and reflective boundaries-- outperforming both in accuracy, with surrogate predictions of the flux in the port plug differing less than 12% from the global transport solution in any given energy bin. The surrogate boundary condition is also demonstrated to be more performant than global transport for the simplified benchmark geometry.
