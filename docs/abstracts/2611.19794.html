Eliciting Research Taste in LLMs through Future Research Direction Choice

Dhruv Trehan, Paras Chopra

arXiv:2611.19794·cs.LG·Published 2026-11-15

Research taste is increasingly discussed in the context of AI scientists, yet remains little studied or measured in LLMs. This judgment matters especially in open-ended research, where a direction must be chosen before a verifiable objective is available. We operationalize research taste through one task of the scientific workflow: choosing which future research direction to pursue given a research goal and current results. Rather than predicting downstream scientometric metrics or treating author choices as objectively correct, we evaluate models against human-authored future-work directions and report author alignment, stable divergence, and unstable choice as behavioral measures of research-direction preference. We construct an order-controlled evaluation of 80 pairwise comparisons from 19 ML papers published between December 2025 and February 2026 across four subdomains. Each query presents a research goal, hypotheses tested with key results, and two candidate future directions: one author-recommended direction and one plausible distractor sampled from a similar paper. Across eight LLMs, author alignment ranges from 45.0% to 67.5%, while stable divergence ranges from 15.0% to 36.2%. These divergences largely persist under similarity controls, prompt ablations, and an author-preference framing check, and inter-model agreement suggests that some non-author preferences are shared across models. We characterize what models diverge toward using transition-category labels and selected reasoning examples, finding recurring movement toward scaling/generalization and trade-offs between methodological and infrastructure/data extensions. We view this as an early descriptive evaluation for eliciting research taste profiles in LLMs, and as a step toward understanding and steering their research preferences as they take on more autonomous roles in novel open-ended scientific discovery.
