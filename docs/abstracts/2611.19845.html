Enhancing Exploration in Ensemble Kalman Inversion via Covariance Inflation and Projected Dropout

Baojun Che, Daniel Zhengyu Huang, Xinying Mao, Weijie Wang

arXiv:2611.19845·math.NA·Published 2026-11-15

Ensemble Kalman inversion (EKI) is a derivative-free and parallelizable method for parameter estimation in Bayesian inverse problems involving expensive forward models. It adapts Kalman filtering by repeatedly assimilating the observation data through an artificial dynamical system with identity state evolution and the parameter-to-observation map as its observation operator. However, its performance can be limited by ensemble collapse and confinement to the affine span of the initial ensemble. We propose an exploration-enhanced EKI framework that combines multiplicative covariance inflation with a projected-dropout correction of the ensemble mean. Inflation helps maintain ensemble spread, while projected dropout enables the mean to explore directions beyond the initial affine span. For linear inverse problems, we provide a discrete-time, finite-ensemble analysis of the resulting method and prove the least-squares objective converges exponentially to its minimum in expectation. Within the initial ensemble subspace, the contraction factor is independent of the conditioning of the inverse problem, and the limiting ensemble covariance provides a low-rank representation of the posterior covariance. Experiments on ill-conditioned linear systems, 100-dimensional nonlinear benchmarks, and a PDE-constrained barotropic flow inverse problem demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method.
