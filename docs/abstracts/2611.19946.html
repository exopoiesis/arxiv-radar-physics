CoSyDelay: Cognitive Symbolic Regression for Traffic Delay Estimation

Lindong Xie, Xiexin Zou, Yang Zhang, Beiyu Song, Zeren Xing, Edward Chung

arXiv:2611.19946·cs.ET·Published 2026-11-15

Trustworthy estimation of traffic delays at signalized intersections is critical for modern transportation systems, as it supports proactive traffic control for congestion mitigation. Existing analytical methods often rely on idealized assumptions that limit their accuracy under complex traffic conditions, whereas data-driven methods may achieve strong estimation performance but usually lack transparent structures, which reduces confidence in their practical application. To address these limitations, we propose CoSyDelay, a cognitive symbolic regression (SR) framework for automatically deriving explicit and physically consistent empirical delay estimation expressions (DEEs). Specifically, CoSyDelay decomposes intersection-level delay estimation into turning-movement-level subproblems, thereby reducing the complexity of the symbolic search. During DEE learning, CoSyDelay constructs domain-informed symbolic inputs from traffic variables. Large language models (LLMs) are employed as symbolic search operators to iteratively generate and refine candidate expression structures, whose movement-specific coefficients are subsequently calibrated using approach-level delay labels. A composite fitness function evaluates the learned DEEs jointly in terms of estimation accuracy and compliance with specified traffic-delay principles. Experimental results on multiple signalized intersections demonstrate that the resulting expressions achieve competitive estimation performance while maintaining physical consistency, thereby supporting trustworthy delay estimation. Source code and datasets are available at https://github.com/ForrestXie9/CoSyDelay.
