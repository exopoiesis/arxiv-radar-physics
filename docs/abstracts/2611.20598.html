Neural Operator Approximation for Yau--Yau Nonlinear Filtering: Stability, Approximation, and Fast Computation

Xiaopei Jiao, Xiuqiong Chen, Stephen S. -T. Yau

arXiv:2611.20598·math.OC·Published 2026-11-16

Since the introduction of the Kalman filter, nonlinear state estimation has become an important topic in areas such as aerospace engineering, sensor design, and target tracking. Within the optimal filtering framework described by the Duncan-Mortensen-Zakai (DMZ) equation, Yau-Yau filtering (YYF) employs an offline-online decomposition: the Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov (FKE) equations, initialized by a prescribed set of basis functions, are solved offline, and the prior density is projected onto these basis functions during the online stage. However, this approach still faces two computational challenges: solving multiple FKEs offline can be computationally expensive, and the online projection becomes costly when filtering is performed repeatedly or in high-dimensional settings. To address these limitations, we develop a neural operator filter (NOF) that directly learns the mapping from the prior density to the posterior density, thereby eliminating both the expensive offline FKE solves and the online projection. The underlying operator is approximated using a Fourier neural operator (FNO). Numerical experiments on several challenging filtering systems verify both the effectiveness and accuracy of our method, and we further provide a rigorous theoretical analysis of its stability and approximation error.
