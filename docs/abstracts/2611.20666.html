Conditional Diffusion Models for Amortized Bayesian System Identification

Mike Hänseroth, Niklas Wahlström, Thomas B. Schön, Thomas Seel, Jan-Hendrik Ewering

arXiv:2611.20666·cs.LG·Published 2026-11-16

Generative diffusion models rapidly solve Bayesian inverse problems, such as image restoration and inpainting, with a single trained model and without retraining for each new observation or task. Bayesian system identification, which typically involves inferring the hidden states and parameters of a dynamical system from measurements, poses a closely related inverse problem. Yet it is often addressed with computationally heavy Monte Carlo methods that must be rerun for every new measurement record. Moreover, these methods are restricted to a single inference task, and their cost grows with the state dimension and the record length. To alleviate these restrictions, we interpret Bayesian system identification in hiddem Markov models (HMMs) as a general Bayesian inverse problem, taking advantage of conditional diffusion models. Specifically, a transformer-based diffusion model is trained once on short segments of simulated trajectories to approximate the joint posterior of states and parameters. Through an attention mask induced by the Markov property and a relative time encoding, it can then be deployed on any new measurement record of custom length. At inference time, the same model can flexibly and rapidly be conditioned for joint state and parameter inference, state smoothing, parameter inference, or reconstructing missing measurements, without retraining. We demonstrate these capabilities on four benchmark systems and examine how the length of the training segments affects state and parameter inference. On records five times longer than the training segments, the model maintains its state accuracy, whereas comparison methods either show a more than tenfold error increase or require ten times as much sampling time.
