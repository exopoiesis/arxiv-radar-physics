A Convergence Analysis of the Random Feature Method for Linear PDEs

Jingrun Chen, Yixin Luo, Xiaojun Peng

arXiv:2611.20951·math.NA·Published 2026-11-16

The Random Feature Method (RFM) distinguishes itself among machine learning approaches to partial differential equations (PDEs) through its superior accuracy and computational efficiency. Nevertheless, the theoretical underpinnings of RFM remain incompletely elucidated. This paper provides a comprehensive analysis of RFM within the framework of kernel methods, focusing on two principal aspects: (i) approximation by finitely many random features in Sobolev norms and the PDE residual seminorm, and (ii) estimation over the induced reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) from finitely many noiseless collocation observations. We relate both problems to kernel ridge regression (KRR) and establish upper and lower bounds in terms of the eigenvalues of the associated integral operators under the respective assumptions. Combining the approximation and estimation lower bounds, we establish a lower bound under simultaneous restrictions on the numbers of random features and collocation observations. We further quantify how activation regularity affects these eigenvalues, obtaining algebraic upper bounds for finitely smooth activations and stretched-exponential upper bounds for analytic activations.
