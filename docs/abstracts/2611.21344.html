TEMPO: Regime-Aware Operator Learning with Locally Adapted POD Bases

Rodion Akinzhala, Alexander Terentyev, Alexey Golubev, Yury Maximov

arXiv:2611.21344·cs.LG·Published 2026-11-16

Operator learning approximates the solution map of a parametric partial differential equation from data, enabling fast prediction at new parameter values without repeated simulation. The dominant approach fits a single global low-rank basis, typically a proper orthogonal decomposition (POD), to all solution snapshots. This fails when the snapshots span qualitatively different solution shapes, from smooth diffusive profiles to near-discontinuous shocks. We propose TEMPO (Trajectory Expectation-Maximization Mixture of POD Operators), a two-phase framework for regime-aware operator learning. In the offline phase, TEMPO recovers latent regimes via expectation-maximization, fitting a dedicated linear or nonlinear basis to each discovered regime. In the online phase, a gating network and per-regime branch networks are trained jointly, routing new inputs to the regimes at inference. We test TEMPO on Burgers' flow, Sod shock tubes, Coanda flow, Darcy flow, Navier-Stokes flow and the heterogeneous Helmholtz equation. With 64 total POD modes, TEMPO gives 18.61% test error on Burgers', compared with 19.61% for global POD. It also gives lower errors on Coanda and Helmholtz.
