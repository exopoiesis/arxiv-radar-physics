Spatially local mini-batching for implicit neural representations

Minseok Choi, Swarup Dhar, Heejae Kwon, Kookjin Lee, Donsub Rim

arXiv:2611.22236·cs.LG·Published 2026-11-17

Neural surrogates based on implicit neural representations (INRs) are typically trained on mini-batches spread over the whole domain, since a generic INR loses accuracy when every point of a batch lies in one small region. We show that low-rank neural representations (LRNRs), INRs whose weight matrices are products of shared bases with coefficients emitted by a small hypernetwork, can be trained with spatially local mini-batches. Each step uses the points of one time snapshot of an adaptive-mesh numerical solution inside one small cell, with snapshots and cells drawn according to the refinement of the mesh. We posit that such training is enabled by the structural property of LRNRs, as a local step reaches the shared bases only through the $r$ coefficients of each layer, and keeps one cell from rewriting the network. By a Kolmogorov-width argument, we show that when restricted to a small cell, the hidden states of LRNRs lie close to a low-dimensional subspace, which suggests the local steps only affect low-rank subspaces of the shared bases. On six hyperbolic-conservation-law problems with solutions computed using adaptive mesh refinement, one local training recipe reaches 1.1-2.3% relative $L^2$ error with a tenth of the parameters of a dense network of the same width. Finally, we provide a comparison by training a hypernetwork-SIREN, a DeepONet, and a Fourier neural operator (FNO) of the same size with the same local batches and training budget. We observe that these baselines reach 16-78% where the LRNR reaches 4.9%. Interestingly, we also observe that local training has a significant effect on the per-cell low-dimensional structure even in a wide generic hypernetwork-SIREN.
