A high-dimensional spatial-physical coupling operator for generalizable super-resolution of three-dimensional turbulent flows

Zengyuan Zuo, Junjun Jiang, Fei Mu, Xubo Wang, Xianming Liu

arXiv:2611.22951·physics.flu-dyn·Published 2026-11-17

Accurate representation of turbulent flows requires simultaneously capturing multi-scale spatial structures and the complex, nonlinear coupling between distinct physical quantities (\textit{e.g.}, velocity components and density). In practice, however, turbulent data are often constrained by low spatial resolution due to hardware limitations and computational costs. While current data-driven super-resolution methods have shown promise by learning mappings via convolutional neural networks (CNNs), they typically treat physical variables as independent channels. This simplified approach neglects intrinsic cross-variable correlations, causing models to struggle with high-fidelity reconstruction, particularly under extremely sparse conditions or in out-of-distribution regimes. To bridge this gap, we propose an architecture-agnostic spatial-physical coupling operator that extends the standard convolution into a joint spatial-variable dimension. Unlike conventional linear channel mixing, our framework constructs a unified spatial-physical representation within CNNs to explicitly capture local inter-variable interactions. By lifting features into a higher-dimensional manifold, this operator significantly enhances the model's representation capacity. Experiments demonstrate that this approach enables strong reconstruction performance and zero-shot generalization, offering a physics-aware inductive bias for super-resolution of three-dimensional turbulence. More broadly, this work suggests that high-dimensional operators offer a promising avenue for modeling intricate multi-scale turbulent flows.
