Gaia's promise to detect compact-object binaries II: Predictions for dormant neutron star binaries in DR4 and beyond

Chirag Chawla, Sourav Chatterjee, Katelyn Breivik

arXiv:2611.23372·astro-ph.SR·Published 2026-11-17

With the fourth data release (DR4) from Gaia imminent, we predict neutron star (NS) binaries with luminous companions (LCs) expected to be detectable in DR4 and the final data release (DR5). We create realistic synthetic populations of NS--LC binaries in the Milky Way using COSMIC, assuming (a) observationally motivated initial distributions of binary properties, (b) metallicity and location dependent star formation history of the MW adopted from the FIRE-2 galaxy m12i, and (c) all relevant binary interaction physics, with the rapid and delayed supernova prescriptions. We estimate the detectable NS--LC population using an approximate Gaia\ selection function based on projected orbital size of the LC as well as gaiamock, which emulates Gaia's multi-epoch astrometry and orbit-fitting pipeline- both after taking into account extinction and reddening. Across supernova models and statistical fluctuations, the former (gaiamock) pipeline predicts $\sim14$--$29$ ($\sim1$--$8$), $4500$--$4700$ ($100$--$110$), and $4800$--$5000$ ($300$--$380$) detectable NS--LC binaries in DR3, DR4, and DR5, respectively. While the orbital-size based pipeline brackets the DR3 yield of NS--LC candidates, the gaiamock prediction falls short, suggesting uncertainties in the modeled binary evolution or contributions from other formation channels. DR4 is expected to directly shed light on the the relative importance of detectable NS--LC binaries from isolated binary evolution vis a vis other channels such as dynamical formation. Interestingly, our models suggest that future releases will also include $22^{+7}_{-5}\ (13^{+5}_{-4})$ and $124^{+14}_{-15}\ (53^{+10}_{-6})$ gaiamock-detectable pulsar--MS binaries in DR4 and DR5, respectively, for the rapid (delayed) model, which are prime targets for radio follow-up.
