How the material world of the brain, as a complex system governed by intermittent dynamics, gives rise to the supposedly "immaterial" world of mental processes such as thought

Yiannis F. Contoyiannis, Alexandros K. Karlis

arXiv:2611.23486·nlin.PS·Published 2026-11-17

In this paper, we argue that the brain is a complex dynamical system in a critical state similar to that of the 3D Ising model. We highlight several properties of the brain, including resonance phenomena within this critical state, long-range autocorrelations consistent with long memory, a hysteresis zone suggesting the presence of an as yet undetected "exotic" type of matter within the brain, and possible quantum phenomena within the microtubules of neural networks. The latter suggests that these structures exhibit a combined form of "logical superposition". In the proposed model, "AND" logic would require two distinct quantum states to be occupied simultaneously. The proposed "OR" logic allows any one of several quantum states to be occupied at a time. Moreover, we propose that kink and antikink solitons could mediate coupling between occupied quantum states in the microtubules and thereby contribute to pattern formation in neural networks. This mechanism may well be what gives the brain the flexibility to support complex thought and other cognitive functions.
