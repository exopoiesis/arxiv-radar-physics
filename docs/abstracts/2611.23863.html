Comparing Data-Driven and Numerical Weather Prediction Forecasts of Extreme Weather: A Review

Gabriele Messori, Hiu-Wang Lau, Chenyu Dong, Gianmarco Mengaldo, Leonardo Olivetti

arXiv:2611.23863·physics.ao-ph·Published 2026-11-18

Data-driven weather forecasting models, chiefly trained on reanalysis data, now match or beat physics-based numerical weather prediction (NWP) models on most headline scores, but whether this advantage extends to extreme weather is contested. We review peer-reviewed studies that directly compare data-driven forecasts with NWP for extremes, from nowcasting to subseasonal lead times. We discuss grey literature in the outlook but do not include it in the formal review. There is good agreement on the fact that data-driven models mostly outperform NWP on temperature extremes, tropical cyclone tracks and the location of synoptic features across time scales. The evidence is mixed for extreme precipitation and feature-based extreme event detection such as extremes related to atmospheric rivers, while it leans toward better NWP performance for estimating event intensity. For record-breaking events that lie outside the training distribution, the evidence is narrower. Nonetheless, both a systematic global evaluation and several case studies find that physics-based models retain the advantage, with data-driven models underestimating both the frequency and the magnitude of records. Hybrid systems that post-process NWP with machine-learning (ML), or couple data-driven forecasts with physics-based models, deliver some of the largest and most consistent gains. We argue that headline scores, verification choices and case selection explain much of the apparent disagreement in the literature, and provide recommendations for a more trustworthy and interpretable evaluation of data-driven models for extreme and record-breaking weather.
