Enabling joint chemical and meteorological data assimilation with an atmospheric foundation model

Eliot J. Kim, Eric J. Mei, Peter D. Ivatt, Lee T. Murray, Alexander J. Turner

arXiv:2611.24020·physics.ao-ph·Published 2026-11-18

The hydroxyl radical (OH) is the main oxidant in the troposphere and accounts for 88% of the methane sink, yet its abundance and trends remain poorly constrained. Satellites observe many of the chemical and meteorological drivers of OH, but assimilating both requires a model that represents chemistry and meteorology together. Existing chemistry-climate models are too expensive for joint data assimilation. Here, we present AuroraChem, a fine-tuned version of the Aurora atmospheric foundation model that jointly simulates meteorology and the GEOS-Chem chemical fields needed to constrain OH. A one-year AuroraChem rollout takes 12 minutes on one GPU. Free rollouts remain stable for one year but do not fully capture seasonal cycles. Using idealized data assimilation, we find that chemical observations can constrain the meteorological state and meteorological observations can constrain the chemical state. Inserting only the chemical fields raises the annual mean spatial correlation of 500 hPa temperature from 0.89 to 0.92 and recovers the moisture and circulation pattern of the Indian summer monsoon. Inserting only the meteorological fields raises the 500 hPa OH correlation from 0.89 to 0.93, close to the 0.94 from inserting the chemical fields themselves, and recovers the OH seasonal cycle. Inserting only NO2 columns sampled at TROPOMI pixels gives an OH correlation of 0.93. These relationships likely reflect learned covariances rather than physical coupling, and covariances are what data assimilation uses to let an observation of one variable update the others. AuroraChem therefore provides a new pathway to joint chemical and meteorological data assimilation.
