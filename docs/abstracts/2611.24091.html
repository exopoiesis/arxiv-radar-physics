Improving Sensitivity to Rare Processes at the LHC with Geodesic-Aware Graph Neural Networks

Benjamin Fuks, A. Hammad, Adil Jueid, Mihoko Nojiri

arXiv:2611.24091·hep-ph·Published 2026-11-18

Rare processes at the Large Hadron Collider often lead to final states with quarks, for which the dominant backgrounds contain the same reconstructed objects as the signal. The colour structure of the hard process, imprinted on the pattern of soft QCD radiation, carries information that is largely left unused by standard analyses. We introduce a geometric representation of this colour flow by mapping hadronic constituents onto the unit sphere through a modified inverse stereographic projection centred on a pair of jets selected as a colour-singlet resonance candidate. The jet separation defines a circular region whose interior and exterior are mapped to opposite hemispheres, allowing a graph neural network to exploit the resulting radiation pattern using geodesic distances. As a benchmark, we consider flavour-changing neutral interactions of the top quark with the Higgs boson at $\sqrt{s}=13.6$~TeV, through associated $tH$ production and $t\bar t$ production with one top quark undergoing a $t\to qH$ decay, where $q=u,c$, focusing on subsequent $H\to b\bar b$ and leptonically top quark decays in both channels. Relative to a baseline graph neural network operating in the $(η,φ)$ plane, the geodesic-aware network improves the expected 95\% confidence-level limits on $\mathrm{BR}(t\to uH)$ and $\mathrm{BR}(t\to cH)$ by about an order of magnitude, to $1.1\times10^{-4}$ and $8.3\times10^{-5}$ for an assumed integrated luminosity of $139~\mathrm{fb}^{-1}$. The approach can also be applied to searches for other colour-singlet resonances decaying into quarks or gluons.
