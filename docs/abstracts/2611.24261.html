Cosmic-ray particles in the TeV range, originating outside the solar system, are partially blocked on their way to Earth by the Moon, producing a deficit in their flux known as the Moon shadow. This work presents a precision measurement of the westward shift of the Moon's shadow using 3.4 years of data from LHAASO's Water Cherenkov Detector Array (WCDA). We use this direct physical measurement, combined with four cosmic-ray composition models, each divided into five mass groups, to calibrate the detector's absolute energy response in the range of 4 - 37 TeV. We quantify the fractional deviation between the calibrated and simulated energy scales by determining a best-fit scale shift parameter of $ε$ =-0.04 $\pm$ 0.03. This yields a 95% confidence interval of -9% to +0.2% for the systematic energy scale deviation. Complementing the simulation based on hadronic interaction model, this geomagnetic calibration provides a data-driven physical validation of the simulated WCDA energy response, effectively bounding systematic uncertainties and refining the overall energy scale.
