Orbital-weighted learning for transition metal complexes

Gabriel Klassen, Guido Falk von Rudorff

arXiv:2611.24421·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-18

Frontier orbital energies are key to the description of molecular light-matter interaction and pose a challenge both for electronic structure calculations and for machine learning approaches, especially for transition metal complexes (TMC). These are highly relevant since the gap between the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) is in the range of visible light. For TMCs, gap position and magnitude are determined by the metal center, its coordination and ligand identity. Since the systems exhibit a wide range of charge and spin states, frontier orbital energies can only be predicted with machine learning models that explicitly incorporate spin and charge. In this work, we show that a semi-empirical orbital population as a weight rather than a feature can increase efficiency for both physics-based and neural-network-based atomic representations. Because orbital populations are highly sensitive to both charge and spin, they offer a general pathway to render existing geometry-based machine learning representations useful for orbital energies. On TMC datasets, our approach reduces the required training data by up to 95 %, and improves physics-based representations to almost match the accuracy of pre-trained neural network representations at 12% of the inference compute cost. This greatly reduces the data and compute requirements to accurately model frontier orbitals in TMCs
