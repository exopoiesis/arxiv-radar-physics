Data-Driven Factored Abstractions of Stochastic Systems

Mahdi Nazeri, Frederik Baymler Mathiesen, Sadegh Soudjani, Alessandro Abate

arXiv:2611.24663·eess.SY·Published 2026-11-18

Formal verification and correct-by-design policy synthesis for stochastic dynamical systems face significant scalability challenges due to the curse of dimensionality. These challenges are exacerbated when the system dynamics are unknown and abstractions must be constructed from data. This paper introduces a data-driven framework for constructing factored interval Markov decision process (fIMDP) abstractions from noisy transition observations. Assuming conditional independence of successor coordinates and known coordinate-wise Lipschitz bounds on the dynamics, the framework estimates marginal transition probabilities rather than joint probabilities, resulting in an exponential reduction in the number of probability entries to be estimated and stored. We establish finite-sample confidence guarantees that the abstraction captures the concrete transition probabilities uniformly over each abstract region, enabling certified lower bounds on satisfaction probabilities for $ω$-regular specifications. Experiments on benchmark systems, including pendulum swing-up, unicycle control, and a four-dimensional planar robot model, demonstrate improvements in certified satisfaction probability lower bounds of up to 25 percentage points compared with a state-of-the-art flat IMDP abstraction method. Our framework required up to 23x less memory and was consistently faster in constructing the abstraction.
