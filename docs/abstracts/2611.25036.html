Simulation-based inference for gravitational-wave cosmology: parameter reconstruction of a first-order electroweak phase transition

Thomas Biekötter, Andrii Dashko, Kateryna Radchenko

arXiv:2611.25036·astro-ph.CO·Published 2026-11-18

The detection of a stochastic gravitational-wave background (SGWB) from a cosmological first- order phase transition would be unambiguous evidence for physics beyond the Standard Model, with a strong electroweak phase transition (EWPT) at LISA as a prime example. Reconstructing the underlying model from such a signal is challenging, since the map from the model parameters to the spectrum is degenerate and strong transitions occupy only small and sparse regions of parameter space. We show that simulation-based inference (SBI), a likelihood-free approach applicable to generic SGWB sources, provides a reliable parameter reconstruction despite these difficulties. We first apply SBI to the thermodynamic parameters characterizing the transition, namely its strength, inverse duration, temperature and bubble wall velocity. As a concrete application, we then consider a strong EWPT in a singlet extension of the Standard Model and reconstruct the model parameters from the resulting SGWB. We find that the SBI posteriors agree with those of a likelihood-based Markov chain Monte Carlo analysis, at negligible cost per data set once the network has been trained. Using the network posterior as the proposal for importance sampling with the exact likelihood further corrects its remaining deviations, at a sampling efficiency several orders of magnitude higher than sampling from the prior. SBI thus offers a fast and flexible framework for the cosmological science case of LISA
