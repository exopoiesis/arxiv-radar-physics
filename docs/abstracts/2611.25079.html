Validation and Physical Characterization of Seven TESS Exoplanets: A Compact Multi-Planet System and Targets for Atmospheric Spectroscopy

Biel Escolà-Rodrigo

arXiv:2611.25079·astro-ph.EP·Published 2026-11-18

The Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) continues to identify thousands of transit candidates that require statistical validation to rule out astrophysical false positives before atmospheric characterization can be pursued. We statistically validate and characterize seven TESS exoplanet candidates orbiting five host stars. As a consistency check of our automated vetting and Bayesian inference pipeline, we include TOI-2392.03, a sub-Neptune companion previously validated in the literature. Our framework combines automated signal retrieval, pixel-level centroid and Gaia DR3 contamination analysis, and false positive probability (FPP) computation with the TRICERATOPS vetting engine. Physical and orbital parameters are derived via nested sampling (juliet/Dynesty), complemented by an empirical mass-radius relation, since no dynamical mass measurements are available. Applying the same procedure to all candidates, we statistically validate six new exoplanets ($\mathrm{FPP} < 0.015$ and $\mathrm{NFPP} < 0.001$), one of which (TOI-7721.01) lies close to the FPP threshold. We report the period ratios of the three-planet system TOI-2392, and identify TOI-7721.01 and TOI-1277.02 as the candidates with the highest Transmission Spectroscopy Metrics (TSM) in our sample, making them targets of interest for atmospheric follow-up with the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) and the upcoming Ariel mission.
