Data-Driven Hamiltonian Under Parametric Uncertainty

Zach Liu, Namhoon Cho, Jason J. Choi

arXiv:2611.25356·eess.SY·Published 2026-11-19

Hamilton--Jacobi (HJ) reachability computes the states from which a system can be kept out of an unsafe region, but it requires knowledge of dynamics. The data-driven Hamiltonian (DDH), under a black-box dynamics setting, lower bounds the Hamiltonian directly from trajectory data, which produces a guaranteed conservative safe set. However, it needs a Lipschitz constant of the dynamics, and its bound loosens with the distance from the data. We consider dynamics that are linear in unknown parameters through a known feature vector, with a Gaussian prior on the parameters, and use this structure to construct a parametric DDH. From a few short trajectories with noisy velocity measurements, the parametric DDH lower-bounds the Hamiltonian at all states and costates with a probability of at least $1-δ$. The safe set obtained by replacing the Hamiltonian with this bound is then contained in the true safe set with the same probability. For control-affine systems, evaluating the bound amounts to a second-order cone program, so it can be solved efficiently within existing grid-based and neural PDE solvers. We demonstrate the approach on a two-state system and a six-state lunar lander.
