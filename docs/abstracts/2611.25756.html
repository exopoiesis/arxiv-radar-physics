Self-Supervised Operator Learning for Channel Estimation in Fluid Antenna Systems

Erqiang Tang, Zixin Wang, Wei Guo, Shenghui Song, Jun Zhang, Khaled B. Letaief

arXiv:2611.25756·cs.IT·Published 2026-11-19

Fluid antenna systems (FAS) have emerged as a promising technology for enhancing wireless communications through adaptive antenna repositioning. Recently, Fourier neural operators (FNOs) have shown strong potential for FAS channel estimation owing to their ability to learn resolution-invariant mappings and generalize across different port densities. However, existing operator learning approaches require densely sampled channel realizations as training labels, whereas a practical receiver only collects noisy pilot observations from a small subset of ports. This mismatch between training requirements and practical channel acquisition poses a major obstacle to deployment. To address this challenge, we develop a self-supervised framework that trains the FNO-based estimator directly from noisy sub-sampled pilots without any labeled channel fields. The proposed objective combines a pilot splitting strategy, which creates supervision at ports hidden from the model input, with a correction based on Stein's unbiased risk estimate (SURE) that eliminates the measurement noise bias. We prove that this objective admits the same minimum mean square error (MMSE) solution as the ideal supervised criterion. Since the loss relies solely on received pilots, the estimator can be continually adapted online on the live pilot stream. Simulation results show that the self-supervised FNO achieves accuracy comparable to supervised training, generalizes zero-shot to unseen port resolutions, and rapidly recovers from distribution shift via online adaptation.
