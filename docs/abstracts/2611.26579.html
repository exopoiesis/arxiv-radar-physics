Trimmed Branching Brownian Motion with Discontinuous Drift

Rami Atar, Leonid Mytnik, Dominic T. Schickentanz

arXiv:2611.26579·math.PR·Published 2026-11-19

We consider a system of branching Brownian particles on the real line with bounded, possibly discontinuous drift $b$, and with a selection rule that removes one particle from the most crowded cells of a uniform partition of the line at each branching event so that the number of particles is preserved. As the number of particles tends to infinity, with the cell size tending to zero (sufficiently slowly), we prove convergence in probability of the empirical measure process to a deterministic limit characterized by the parabolic free obstacle problem $$\partial_tu=\tfrac12\partial_{xx}u-\partial_x(bu)+u-β.$$ Here the density $u$ has unit mass. Disintegrating the removal measure $β$ as $β_t(\mathrm{d} x)\mathrm{d} t$, the probability measure $β_t$ is supported on the spatial maximizers of $u(t,\cdot)$ for a.e. $t$. We establish existence and uniqueness of weak solutions in a suitable class of continuous densities, extending earlier results about this PDE to discontinuous drifts. We then consider an inverse problem: Given a target removal measure, find a drift for which the PDE admits a stationary solution with that removal measure. We show that this problem is solvable for every compactly supported Borel probability measure.
