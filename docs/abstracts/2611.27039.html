Measuring and predicting plasma blob influence on downstream transport through paired simulations

J. C. Huang, Z. S. Qu, R. Varennes, X. Garbet, C. G. Wan, C. Guet, D. Niyato, K. Lim, V. Grandgirard

arXiv:2611.27039·physics.plasm-ph·Published 2026-11-20

We use two-dimensional TOKAM2D simulations of flux-driven turbulence to measure how much a single plasma blob affects particle transport farther out in the scrape-off layer. We evolve the same turbulent state twice, weakening the density and potential variations of one blob in one of the two simulations. The resulting difference in outward particle flux, integrated over time in each poloidal sector of a downstream band, defines the blob's influence vector $α$. Its magnitude falls steeply with distance from the band and generally increases with blob size and density content. Even blobs with similar bulk properties can differ in influence by orders of magnitude. A graph neural network that uses the recent evolution of the fields in and around each blob predicts much of this difference for pairs of otherwise similar blobs. To see how the difference develops, we compare the downstream density and radial velocity in the two simulations. For both strongly and weakly influential blobs in the pairs examined, changes in radial velocity account for most of the local change in outward flux. In the stronger responses, the density and flow contributions tend to reinforce each other in the same sectors at the same times.
