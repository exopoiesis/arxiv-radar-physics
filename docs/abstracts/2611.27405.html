Learning effective stochastic predator and prey dynamics across physical and biological systems from limited data

J. C. Huang, Z. S. Qu, R. Varennes, X. Garbet, C. G. Wang, C. Guet, D. Niyato, K. Lim, V. Grandgirard

arXiv:2611.27405·physics.plasm-ph·Published 2026-11-20

Constructing an effective low-dimensional stochastic model from sparse observations requires separating deterministic drift from state-dependent fluctuations when the governing interaction law is unknown. We formulate an adaptive neural stochastic differential equation with topology constraints (TC-AdaNSDE) for two-variable predator--prey dynamics. To regularize the inverse problem when data are scarce, the model combines flexible drift and correlated diffusion fields with density-adaptive local Kramers--Moyal moment estimates and soft phase-space constraints reflecting boundary saddle behavior near extinction and local stability near coexistence. Using a known nonlinear stochastic system, we first show that short-time trajectory error cannot assess diffusion accuracy on its own and must be paired with distributional measures of one-step transitions and long-time phase-space occupancy. We then apply this framework to macroscopic turbulence and zonal flow energies reduced from Hasegawa--Wakatani plasma simulations and to short ecological time series. In the sparse-data regime, TC-AdaNSDE reproduces both short-horizon evolution and long-time invariant energy distributions where deterministic reductions collapse and unconstrained stochastic fits diverge, while recovering physically interpretable drift and fluctuation fields across both physical and biological systems.
