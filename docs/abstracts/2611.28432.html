A Bayesian Mechanistic Model of Spatiotemporal Dynamics in Invasive Tree Species

Jiaqi Chen, Huijing Du, Yawen Guan

arXiv:2611.28432·stat.ME·Published 2026-11-21

Modeling the spatiotemporal dynamics of invasive species requires frameworks that represent spread and growth mechanisms explicitly while accommodating the distributional features of ecological data. We propose a Bayesian mechanistic framework for invasive tree cover that combines an ecological diffusion--logistic growth partial differential equation (PDE) with a zero-inflated binomial observation model. The diffusion and growth coefficients are modeled as functions of environmental covariates, enabling direct inference on the ecological processes driving encroachment, and the zero-inflation component separates structural absence from low observed cover. To enable scalable Bayesian inference on satellite-resolution grids, we derive a homogenized equation for ecological diffusion with spatially varying diffusion and growth coefficients, which reduces computation while preserving large-scale spatial structure. Applied to satellite-derived tree cover in a subregion of the Nebraska Sandhills, where eastern redcedar is encroaching on grassland, the model indicates that elevation is strongly associated with where tree cover accumulates but only weakly with its local growth rate, whereas distance to the nearest stream is associated with both. Compared with a generalized additive model, a spatial hierarchical model fitted via INLA, and a regression tree, the proposed method directly estimates these processes as interpretable functions of environmental covariates and gives the best-calibrated predictive uncertainty.
