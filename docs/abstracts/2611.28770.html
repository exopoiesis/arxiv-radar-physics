Cure-Fraction Random Survival Forests: Separating Cure from Latency

Pedro Luiz Ramos, Lucas Osses, Debajyoti Sinha

arXiv:2611.28770·stat.ME·Published 2026-11-21

In many cancer and clinical studies, the population under study has a positive fraction of long-term survivors, motivating survival models that distinguish probability of long-term survival (cure fraction) from the latency of the subgroup that is susceptible to the event. Existing survival trees and forests partition data using criteria that emphasize differences in the ranks of survival times and therefore need not identify covariates associated with cure probability. We introduce cure-fraction based survival trees (CBST) that recursively partition the covariate space using contrasts in tail survival probability supported by adequate follow-up. The resulting ensemble forest targets long-term survival heterogeneity directly rather than ranking of the survival times. We establish consistency for the induced tail-survival target and derive conditions under which our procedure consistently recovers cure-rate related partitions and cure probabilities. The analysis clarifies the separate roles of follow-up maturity, localization, and residual-tail bias in identifying cure effects and supporting statistical inference. We further develop variance estimation and asymptotic results and uncertainty quantification for predictions from the ensemble forest. Simulations show that the cure-fraction based splitting criterion can improve recovery of cure heterogeneity relative to a log-rank based forest when cure incidence and latency are driven by different covariates. Analyses of temporally held-out cohorts from the Sao Paulo Hospital Cancer Registry demonstrate that our method produces stable long-term survival risk groups while preserving substantial late follow-up support. The proposed framework provides a nonparametric approach to learning and interpreting cure heterogeneity in settings where long-term survival is the primary scientific objective.
