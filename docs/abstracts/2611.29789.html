Tabular Foundation Models for Weighted Regression and Classification

Junwen Xia, Yan He, Dehan Kong, Zhenhua Lin

arXiv:2611.29789·stat.ML·Published 2026-11-22

Weighted regression and classification have applications ranging from missing-data analysis and causal inference to insurance pricing and particle physics. Existing weighted methods must be fitted and tuned on each dataset, which is costly for flexible nonlinear learners. Tabular foundation models avoid this cost. A Transformer is pretrained once on many simulated datasets and then predicts on a new dataset by in-context learning, using its observations as context without further fitting. Recent models such as TabPFN-3.5 surpass tuned conventional methods, but they cannot use sample weights. We develop a general pipeline that adapts such models to weighted prediction through weighted attention, a normalized weighted loss, and pretraining on synthetic weighted prediction tasks from missing-data problems. Applied to TabPFN-3.5, it yields WeightedPFN, which performs strongly on real datasets, both in missing-data problems and in out-of-distribution causal tasks, while running about as fast as linear baselines. We also establish a statistical theory showing that a suitably pretrained weighted Transformer attains the nonparametric minimax rate for weighted regression on new datasets without further fitting.
