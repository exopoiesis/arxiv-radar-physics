Model-Assisted Probability of Detection Studies for Image-Based Human Inspection

Qizheng Xia, William Q. Meeker, Joseph N. Gray, Qing Li

arXiv:2611.30032·stat.AP·Published 2026-11-23

Establishing a probability of detection (POD) curve is important for assessing the reliability of components in aging systems. Traditional POD studies rely on physical specimens containing representative flaws and human inspectors who record detection results, making such studies costly. This paper demonstrates how appropriately validated physics-based simulation can substantially reduce, and in suitable applications potentially replace, physical POD experiments. We use XRSIM, an X-ray radiographic simulator, to generate weld images containing cracks of varying size, morphology, and location. Flaw characteristics, locations, and the number of flaws per image were randomized, with images containing zero to three flaws. Each simulated flaw is treated as an observational unit contributing flaw-level hit-miss responses. To capture human inspection variability, the images were presented in random order to five inspectors, each of whom inspected the images twice. The resulting data were analyzed using a repeated-measures binary-regression model accounting for flaw-to-flaw, inspector-to-inspector, flaw/inspector-combination, and inspection-to-inspection variability. Bayesian inference was used to estimate the population-mean POD curve, inspector-specific POD curves, and associated statistical uncertainty. Results show that flaw-to-flaw variability dominates, with appreciable differences among inspectors. The study provides a framework for combining physics-based image simulation with controlled human inspection to estimate POD while reducing the experimental burden of physical specimen fabrication.
