Geospatial foundation models and deep learning for climate resilience: a review of transfer, uncertainty and decision readiness

Parastoo Farajpoor, Mohammadreza Narimani

arXiv:2611.30198·eess.IV·Published 2026-11-23

Geospatial foundation models can reuse information learned from large Earth-observation archives, but their value for climate resilience depends on whether that information transfers to unfamiliar events, regions and observing conditions. This review connects advances in deep learning with the evidence needed for hazard monitoring and early warning. A descriptive audit of 137 full-text hazard-application studies is interpreted alongside a separate corpus of 37 model and benchmark records. Ninety-eight application studies reported an out-of-distribution experiment, whereas headline evaluations included held-out events in 31 studies, regions in 25 and sensors in only two. None of the ten studies coded for geospatial foundation-model fine-tuning used sensor holdout as a headline test. Uncertainty-related methods were recorded in 16 studies, including two with explicitly calibrated probabilistic output. These overlapping counts describe evaluation practice within the coded corpus, not a ranking of model families. We distinguish Earth-observation representation learning from atmospheric forecasting and examine how labels, observability, forecast lead time and decision requirements constrain their contribution across flood, wildfire, drought, heat, cryosphere and cascading-hazard applications. We then develop a decision-backward evaluation framework: define the action and information available when it must be taken, reproduce the relevant shift, retain a matched task-specific baseline, and report misses, false alarms, reliability and end-to-end latency. The framework turns transferability from a broad model claim into a testable requirement for climate adaptation and risk management.
