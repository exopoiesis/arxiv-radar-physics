Reconstructing the depth of first interaction in extensive air showers from fluorescence detector profiles with a physics-motivated neural network

Marcos Olegario, Vitor de Souza

arXiv:2611.30661·astro-ph.HE·Published 2026-11-23

We present a physics-motivated neural network that reconstructs, event by event, the depth of the first interaction of ultra-high-energy cosmic rays from longitudinal profiles measured by fluorescence detectors. The network couples a convolutional encoder to a decoder that implements the Gaisser-Hillas function and predicts quantiles of the target, providing a per-event uncertainty estimate. It is trained and evaluated on four million proton- and iron-induced showers between $10^{18}$ and $10^{20}$ eV, simulated with the hadronic interaction models EPOS LHC-R and Sibyll 2.3d and processed by an end-to-end simulation of the fluorescence detector response. Despite detector effects, the reconstruction retains useful information for protons, achieving a mean absolute error of about 21 g$\,$cm$^{-2}$ for primary energies around $10^{19}$ eV, whereas for iron most of the information is lost. The reconstructed distributions preserve the exponential shape of the true ones, so the proton-air interaction length can be recovered from their tail with a model-related systematic uncertainty of approximately 4%. This value is limited mainly by the choice of the hadronic interaction model and is nearly insensitive to iron contamination. These results show that the depth of the first interaction can be reconstructed under realistic detection conditions, offering a complementary route to measurements of the proton-air cross section and mass-composition studies.
