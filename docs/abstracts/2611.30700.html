Sequential Monitoring of Image Streams Using Energy Statistics

Shreetama Bhuniya, Sourav De, Anik Roy, Partha Sarathi Mukherjee

arXiv:2611.30700·stat.AP·Published 2026-11-23

Sequential monitoring of image data is a high-velocity, high-dimensional big data problem in statistics and machine learning, with wide applications in manufacturing industries, satellite imaging, medical diagnostics, and remote sensing. This is a challenging task because image intensity functions are often contaminated by noise and contain discontinuities and other complicated spatial structures. In the literature, a vast majority of image-monitoring methods rely on changes in image intensity values to detect out-of-control images. However, such approaches are often unreasonable in applications where insignificant changes in image contrast or background intensity should not indicate an out-of-control image as long as the boundaries of the image objects remain unchanged. In this article, we propose a nonparametric Phase II image-monitoring procedure based on energy distance that directly monitors changes in the distribution of image boundaries. The proposed method constructs a moving-window energy-distance-based control statistic by comparing the distribution of recently observed image boundaries with that of a fixed in-control reference sample. The central idea behind the proposed boundary-based approach is twofold: first, to reduce the dimensionality of high-resolution images by retaining relevant edge information, and second, to provide a robust monitoring procedure that is less sensitive to insignificant smooth changes in image intensity. The proposed construction offers a computationally efficient distributional approach to image monitoring without requiring a parametric model for out-of-control images. Theoretical justifications and superior performance in numerical comparisons ensure that the proposed control chart can be useful to many researchers and practitioners.
