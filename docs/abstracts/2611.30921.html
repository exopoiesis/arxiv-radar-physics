Quantifying Errors in Chemical Kinetics

Skyla B. White, Paul B. Rimmer, Miles Cramner, Alex T. Archibald

arXiv:2611.30921·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-11-23

The physical structure of kinetic measurements determines the correct statistical framework for quantifying their uncertainties, yet this framework has never been explicitly derived or systematically applied in the chemical kinetics literature. Here, we derive a forward model for kinetic observations from a minimal set of stated assumptions about the measurement process, and identify the correct statistical treatment for each kinetic parameter as an explicit consequence of that model. We then audit the literature pertaining to a well-studied reaction, examining the kinetics of permanganate oxidation, to establish the scale of deviation from the correct framework. We find that of 330 measurements, 35.8% carry no uncertainty quantification of any kind and none are accompanied by a full propagation of all uncertainties. To demonstrate the practical consequences of departing from the correct framework, we reanalyse a representative kinetic dataset, first checking whether the data are consistent with the forward model and then applying the correct procedures. We find that the impacts are modest for the dataset examined, as expected for quantities determined in a low-uncertainty regime, but that uncertainties in activation energy are systematically underestimated when observed rather than assumed measurement precision is used as the basis for regression weights. We highlight that these consequences become substantially more significant at the limits of current experimental methodologies, where measurement uncertainty is large and the correct distributional treatment can no longer be approximated by standard approaches. Based on this, we propose a set of minimum recommendations for error reporting in chemical kinetics that follow directly from our forward model.
