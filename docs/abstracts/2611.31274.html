GeoCANO: A Geometry-Conditioned Adaptive Neural Operator for Physical-Field Prediction on Varying Domains

Biao Chen, Xinrui Zhou, Xianlin Jin, Jing Wang, Jifa Zhang, Chen Zhai, Wei Liu, Miao Zhang

arXiv:2611.31274·eess.IV·Published 2026-11-15

Industrial design requires repeated evaluation of candidate configurations. Integral performance measures alone do not resolve critical local phenomena, while high-fidelity simulation remains costly. Existing physical-field surrogates often couple geometry representation and coordinate evaluation through prescribed mesh topologies, limiting reuse across resolutions. We propose the Geometry-Conditioned Adaptive Neural Operator (GeoCANO), which maps a sampled complete three-dimensional geometry to a coordinate-dependent field function. An attention-based encoder extracts a geometry state from unordered surface coordinates. A multiresolution axis-factorized hash encoding and query-wise sparse mixture-of-experts decoder enhance multiscale expressiveness. GeoCANO constructs the field representation before introducing output coordinates, allowing one geometry encoding to be reused across regions without rebuilding the graph for each output topology. We evaluated GeoCANO on transonic aerodynamic-field prediction over complete aircraft configurations using a Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes dataset of 10,484 wing-body-nacelle geometries. For unseen configurations within the sampled design family, GeoCANO outperformed baselines in complete-surface prediction, high-gradient pressure reconstruction, and pressure-drag recovery. It reduced pressure-coefficient mean absolute error by 77.9% relative to the strongest baseline and retained an advantage over a finite range of wall-normal distances. Ablation studies showed that geometry-modulated coordinate encoding achieved comparable accuracy with substantially fewer parameters than conventional three-dimensional hash encoding. Routing analysis indicated spatially structured, query-dependent expert specialization. These results demonstrate a reusable continuous-coordinate representation for rapid physical-field evaluation over varying geometries.
