Variational Principles and Machine Learning: a Numerical Analysis and PDE perspective

Charalambos G. Makridakis

arXiv:2611.34314·math.NA·Published 2026-11-25

Variational principles have played a fundamental role in the development of modern mathematics, physics, and scientific computing. From the principles of least action in mechanics to the calculus of variations and the variational formulation of partial differential equations, they have provided a powerful framework for understanding complex problems and designing reliable numerical methods. In recent years, Machine Learning has introduced a new computational paradigm, in which highly expressive nonlinear function spaces and large-scale minimisation problems play a central role. In these notes, we explore the connection between these two frameworks. We develop a variational and numerical-analysis perspective on Machine Learning algorithms, focusing in particular on their formulation as nonlinear minimisation problems. We investigate how classical concepts such as stability, consistency, approximability, and compactness acquire new interpretations in this setting, and how the variational structure of the underlying continuous problem can govern the behaviour of neural-network approximations. This perspective provides a mathematical framework for understanding existing ML algorithms while also suggesting new approaches to their design. In particular, alternative and strongly coercive variational principles can lead to more stable, reliable, and potentially more efficient computational methods.
