NASA's Pandora SmallSat Mission: Gaussian Processes for Simulated Stellar Heterogeneity Corrections under Imperfect Stellar Models

Zoe Ko, Peter McGill, Benjamin V. Rackham, Dániel Apai, Aishwarya R. Iyer, Jason F. Rowe, Luis Welbanks, Suman Saha, Yoav Rotman, Elisa V. Quintana, Jessie L. Dotson, Knicole D. Colón, Thomas Barclay, Christina Hedges, Emily A. Gilbert, Néstor Espinoza, Trevor O. Foote, Rae Holcomb, Benjamin J. Hord, Brett M. Morris, Joe P. Renaud

arXiv:2611.34720·astro-ph.SR·Published 2026-11-25

Stellar contamination is the primary bottleneck limiting robust atmospheric characterization of exoplanets orbiting active K- and M-dwarfs with James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) transmission spectroscopy. Launched in January 2026, NASA's Pandora SmallSat mission addresses this challenge through long-term, simultaneous visible photometry and near-infrared spectroscopy of planet-hosting stars, providing the data to disentangle planetary and stellar signals. However, conventional stellar-contamination corrections rely on stellar atmosphere models, making them vulnerable to model inaccuracies that can impede accurate inferences. Here, we investigate how Pandora data can be used to mitigate stellar contamination without an accurate host star model. We present a Gaussian-process-aided stellar retrieval framework that learns wavelength-dependent deviations from current stellar model grids. We propagate stellar contamination factors directly from the stellar retrieval into the planetary atmospheric retrievals, allowing the atmospheric inference to marginalize over stellar-contamination uncertainty without relying on a biased stellar model. Tests using simulated Pandora observations with realistic stellar model mismatch show that this framework consistently improves the recovery of disk-integrated stellar spectra. For a WASP-69 b-like system, this approach can reduce model-driven biases in inferred atmospheric properties, though degeneracies among stellar and atmospheric parameters can leave residual biases. This flexible, data-driven framework is a promising approach toward accurate stellar-contamination corrections and more robust atmospheric inference even when host-star model grids are inaccurate.
